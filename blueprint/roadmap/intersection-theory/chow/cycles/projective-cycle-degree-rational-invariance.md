# The degree of cycles on projective space is invariant under rational equivalence

Let $k$ be algebraically closed and $0\le m\le n$. For an $m$-dimensional
cycle $\alpha=\sum_i n_i[Y_i]$ on $\mathbb{P}^n_k$, define

$\deg\alpha=\sum_i n_i\deg Y_i$,

where $\deg Y_i$ is the projective degree of the integral subvariety $Y_i$,
read off its Hilbert polynomial. With rational equivalence defined through
divisors on normalizations, $\deg$ vanishes on
$\operatorname{Rat}^{n-m}(\mathbb{P}^n_k)$: for every integral
$(m+1)$-dimensional subvariety $V\subseteq\mathbb{P}^n_k$ with normalization
$\nu:\widetilde V\to\mathbb{P}^n_k$ and every nonzero rational function $g$ on
$\widetilde V$,

$\deg\nu_*\operatorname{div}(g)=0$.

Hence the degree descends to a homomorphism
$\deg:A^{n-m}(\mathbb{P}^n_k)\to\mathbb{Z}$. For $m = 0$ this is the degree
$A^n(\mathbb{P}^n_k) \to \mathbb{Z}$ of Appendix A.1 (p.426), which Hartshorne shows
is well defined by (II, 6.10).

Write $g=F/G$ with $F,G$ forms of the same degree $e$, neither vanishing
identically on $V$, and factor $F=\prod_j F_j$ and $G=\prod_l G_l$ into
irreducible forms, repeated factors allowed. For a prime divisor $Y$ of $V$,
the coefficient of $[Y]$ in $\nu_*\operatorname{div}(g)$ is
$\sum_{\widetilde Y\mapsto Y}\operatorname{ord}_{\widetilde Y}(g)\,[K(\widetilde Y):K(Y)]$.
Choose a linear form $L$ not vanishing on $Y$. By the length formula for
orders through the normalization, applied to the one-dimensional local ring
$\mathcal{O}_{V,Y}$ and the elements $F_j/L^{\deg F_j}$ and $G_l/L^{\deg G_l}$,
and additivity of orders, this equals
$\sum_j i(V,Z(F_j);Y)-\sum_l i(V,Z(G_l);Y)$, a term being $0$ when $Y$ is not
a component of the intersection; identifying the length over
$\mathcal{O}_{V,Y}$ with the multiplicity at the homogeneous prime of $Y$
compares lengths over the degree-zero localization
$S(V)_{(\mathfrak{p}_Y)} = \mathcal{O}_{V,Y}$ and over the ordinary localization
$S(V)_{\mathfrak{p}_Y}$. Bézout for $V$ and each irreducible hypersurface $Z(F_j)$, $Z(G_l)$
then gives
$\deg\nu_*\operatorname{div}(g)=\sum_j\deg F_j\deg V-\sum_l\deg G_l\deg V=e\deg V-e\deg V=0$.
