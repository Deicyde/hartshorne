# Projective algebraic sets are finite unions of their components

Every projective algebraic set $Y$ is the union of its finitely many
irreducible components. Each component is a projective variety, and every
point of $Y$ belongs to at least one component.

This is the projective finite decomposition of Hartshorne's Exercise
I.2.5(b), used in the extension of Bézout's theorem to reducible plane
curves in Remark I.7.8.2.
