# The projective closure of an affine variety

Identify $\mathbb{A} ^{n}$ with the chart $U_{0} \subseteq \mathbb{P} ^{n}$ by $\varphi _{0}$. For an affine variety
$Y \subseteq \mathbb{A} ^{n}$, the *projective closure* $\overline{Y}$ is the closure of $Y$ in $\mathbb{P} ^{n}$, and
$I(\overline{Y} )$ is the homogeneous ideal generated by $\beta (I(Y))$, the homogenisations of
the elements of $I(Y)$ (Exercise 2.9(a)).

Adopted from §2 because [Proposition 4.9](birational-hypersurface.md) needs it:
the hypersurface that proposition produces is built in $\mathbb{A} ^{r+1}$ and has to be
moved into $\mathbb{P} ^{r+1}$.

## What is and is not true

$\overline{Y}$ is a projective variety: it is closed by construction, and irreducible
because $Y$ is and closure preserves irreducibility. It contains $Y$ as a dense
open subset, so by [Exercise 2.7(b)](../projective-varieties/projective-dimension.md)
the two have the same dimension, and by
[Corollary 4.5](birational-criterion.md) they are birational — indeed they
already share the open subset $Y$.

The ideal statement needs care in one direction only. That
the ideal generated by the homogenizations of $I(Y)$ is contained in $I(\overline Y)$ is immediate: a homogenised element vanishes on
$Y$, hence on its closure. The converse is the content, and it is the
homogenisation–dehomogenisation dictionary again: a homogeneous $g \in I(\overline{Y} )$
dehomogenises to $\alpha (g) \in I(Y)$, and $g$ differs from $\beta (\alpha (g))$ only by a power
of $x_{0}$, which is a unit on the chart.

Hartshorne's part (b) — that generators of $I(Y)$ need not homogenise to
generators of $I(\overline{Y} )$, with the twisted cubic as the counterexample — is a
warning rather than a result and is not claimed here. It is worth stating the
warning anyway: the ideal is generated by $\beta$ of *every* element of $I(Y)$, not
by $\beta$ of a generating set.
