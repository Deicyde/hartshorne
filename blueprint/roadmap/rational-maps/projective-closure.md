# The projective closure of an affine variety

Identify `𝔸ⁿ` with the chart `U₀ ⊆ ℙⁿ` by `φ₀`. For an affine variety
`Y ⊆ 𝔸ⁿ`, the *projective closure* `Ȳ` is the closure of `Y` in `ℙⁿ`, and
`I(Ȳ)` is the homogeneous ideal generated by `β(I(Y))`, the homogenisations of
the elements of `I(Y)` (Exercise 2.9(a)).

Adopted from §2 because [Proposition 4.9](birational-hypersurface.md) needs it:
the hypersurface that proposition produces is built in `𝔸^{r+1}` and has to be
moved into `ℙ^{r+1}`.

## What is and is not true

`Ȳ` is a projective variety: it is closed by construction, and irreducible
because `Y` is and closure preserves irreducibility. It contains `Y` as a dense
open subset, so by [Exercise 2.7(b)](../projective-varieties/projective-dimension.md)
the two have the same dimension, and by
[Corollary 4.5](birational-criterion.md) they are birational — indeed they
already share the open subset `Y`.

The ideal statement needs care in one direction only. That
`Ideal.span (β(I(Y))) ⊆ I(Ȳ)` is immediate: a homogenised element vanishes on
`Y`, hence on its closure. The converse is the content, and it is the
homogenisation–dehomogenisation dictionary again: a homogeneous `g ∈ I(Ȳ)`
dehomogenises to `α(g) ∈ I(Y)`, and `g` differs from `β(α(g))` only by a power
of `x₀`, which is a unit on the chart.

Hartshorne's part (b) — that generators of `I(Y)` need not homogenise to
generators of `I(Ȳ)`, with the twisted cubic as the counterexample — is a
warning rather than a result and is not claimed here. It is worth stating the
warning anyway: the ideal is generated by `β` of *every* element of `I(Y)`, not
by `β` of a generating set.
