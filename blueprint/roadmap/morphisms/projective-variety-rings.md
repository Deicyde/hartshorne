# The rings of a projective variety

Let `Y ⊆ ℙⁿ` be a projective variety with homogeneous coordinate ring `S(Y)`.
Then (Theorem 3.4):

- `𝒪(Y) = k`;
- `𝒪_P = S(Y)_(𝔪_P)` for `P ∈ Y`, where `𝔪_P` is generated by the homogeneous
  `f ∈ S(Y)` vanishing at `P`;
- `K(Y) ≅ S(Y)_((0))`.

Parts (b) and (c) come from the affine case: on `Yᵢ = Y ∩ Uᵢ` there is an
isomorphism `A(Yᵢ) ≅ S(Y)_(xᵢ)`, and transitivity of localization converts
Theorem 3.2 into the graded statements.

Part (a) is the one with real content and no affine analogue. A global regular
`f` lies in every `A(Yᵢ)`, so `xᵢ^{Nᵢ} f ∈ S(Y)` for each `i`; choosing
`N ≥ Σ Nᵢ` gives `S(Y)_N · f ⊆ S(Y)_N`, iterating gives `S(Y)[f]` inside a
finitely generated `S(Y)`-module, so `f` is integral over `S(Y)`. Taking
degree-zero components of an integral equation puts the coefficients in
`S(Y)₀ = k`, and `k` is algebraically closed, so `f ∈ k`.

That `𝒪(Y) = k` is the structural reason projective varieties need sheaf
cohomology rather than global functions, and it is the motivation Hartshorne
gives for Chapters II and III.
