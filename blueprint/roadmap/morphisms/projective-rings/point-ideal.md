# The homogeneous prime at a point

`𝔪_P`, the ideal of `S(Y)` generated by the homogeneous elements vanishing at a
point `P ∈ Y`, together with the three facts Theorem 3.4(b) needs of it: it is
prime, it is homogeneous, and it misses the class of `xᵢ` when `P` lies in the
chart `Uᵢ`.

Hartshorne names `𝔪_P` in the statement of 3.4(b) and says nothing further about
it. The three facts are exactly what
[the graded comparison](graded-localization.md) requires of its prime, and none
of them is free — which is the difference between parts (b) and (c), since the
`(0)` of part (c) needs no construction at all.

## Working upstairs

The construction pushes `J({P})` down to `S(Y)` rather than describing `𝔪_P`
directly, and every proof stays upstairs. That is possible because
`J(Y) ≤ J({P})` for `P ∈ Y`, so the quotient map neither adds nor loses
membership: an element of `S(Y)` lies in `𝔪_P` exactly when any representative
lies in `J({P})`.

Primality then reduces to Hartshorne's quoted fact that a homogeneous ideal can
be tested on homogeneous elements. For a single point the test is that a product
of two values in `k` vanishes only if one does, so it is primality of `(0)` in a
field and nothing more. Homogeneity is that `𝔪_P` is spanned by images of
homogeneous polynomials. And `xᵢ ∉ 𝔪_P` is that `xᵢ` does not vanish at a point
of the chart, which is the definition of the chart.
