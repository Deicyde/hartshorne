# The homogeneous prime at a point

$\mathfrak{m} _P$, the ideal of $S(Y)$ generated by the homogeneous elements vanishing at a
point $P \in Y$, together with the three facts [Theorem I.3.4(b)](projective-local-ring.md) needs of it: it is
prime, it is homogeneous, and it misses the class of $x_{i}$ when $P$ lies in the
chart $U_{i}$.

Hartshorne names $\mathfrak{m} _P$ in the statement of 3.4(b) and says nothing further about
it. The three facts are exactly what
[the graded comparison](graded-localization.md) requires of its prime, and none
of them is free — which is the difference between parts (b) and (c), since the
$(0)$ of part (c) needs no construction at all.

## Working upstairs

The construction pushes $J(\{P\})$ down to $S(Y)$ rather than describing $\mathfrak{m} _P$
directly, and every proof stays upstairs. That is possible because
$J(Y) \le J(\{P\})$ for $P \in Y$, so the quotient map neither adds nor loses
membership: an element of $S(Y)$ lies in $\mathfrak{m} _P$ exactly when any representative
lies in $J(\{P\})$.

Primality then reduces to Hartshorne's quoted fact that a homogeneous ideal can
be tested on homogeneous elements. For a single point the test is that a product
of two values in $k$ vanishes only if one does, so it is primality of $(0)$ in a
field and nothing more. Homogeneity is that $\mathfrak{m} _P$ is spanned by images of
homogeneous polynomials. And $x_{i} \notin \mathfrak{m} _P$ is that $x_{i}$ does not vanish at a point
of the chart, which is the definition of the chart.
