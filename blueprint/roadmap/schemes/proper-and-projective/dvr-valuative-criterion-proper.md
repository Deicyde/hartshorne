# DVR-valuative criterion for properness

Let $f : X \longrightarrow Y$ be a finite-type morphism of Noetherian schemes. Then $f$
is proper if and only if every valuative commutative square whose valuation
ring is a discrete valuation ring has a unique lift.

The forward implication restricts the full valuative criterion. Conversely,
uniqueness first gives separatedness by the DVR criterion. Since $f$ is
quasi-compact, the valuative criterion for universal closedness reduces
universal closedness to the existence of a lift in every valuative commutative
square, now with an arbitrary valuation ring $R$ of a field $K$. Let
$x \in X$ be the image of $\operatorname{Spec} K$, with
$\kappa (x) \hookrightarrow K$, let $V \subseteq Y$ be an affine open containing
the image of the closed point of $\operatorname{Spec} R$, and cover
$f^{-1}(V)$ by finitely many affine opens $U_{1}, \dots , U_{n}$. If for some
$U_{j} \ni x$ the image of $\mathcal{O}(U_{j})$ in $K$ lies in $R$, then
$\operatorname{Spec} R \to \operatorname{Spec} \mathcal{O}(U_{j}) = U_{j}$ is a
lift. Otherwise choose, for each $U_{j} \ni x$, a section
$b_{j} \in \mathcal{O}(U_{j})$ whose value $\beta_{j} \in \kappa (x)$ does not
lie in $R$; then $\beta_{j}^{-1}$ lies in the maximal ideal of $R$. The
subring $D \subseteq \kappa (x)$ generated by the image of $\mathcal{O}(V)$ and
the $\beta_{j}^{-1}$ is Noetherian, being a finitely generated algebra over the
Noetherian ring $\mathcal{O}(V)$. Its localization $A$ at the contraction of
the maximal ideal of $R$ is a Noetherian local domain whose maximal ideal is
nonzero and contains every $\beta_{j}^{-1}$, and $\kappa (x)$ is a finitely
generated field extension of the fraction field of $A$ because $f$ is of finite
type. Choose a DVR $R'$ of $\kappa (x)$ dominating $A$. The valuative square
formed by $\operatorname{Spec} \kappa (x) \to X$ and
$\operatorname{Spec} R' \to \operatorname{Spec} \mathcal{O}(V) \to Y$ has a
lift, which sends the closed point into some $U_{j}$; this $U_{j}$ contains
$x$, so $\beta_{j} \in R'$, while $\beta_{j}^{-1}$ lies in the maximal ideal
of $R'$, a contradiction. No base change is needed, so every ring that occurs
is Noetherian.
