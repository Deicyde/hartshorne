# Divisors of homogeneous fractions on Proj of a graded domain

Let $S = \bigoplus_{d \ge 0} S_d$ be a graded integral domain, generated as an
$S_0$-algebra by $S_1$, with $S_1 \ne 0$, and suppose $X = \operatorname{Proj} S$
satisfies $(*)$. For nonzero homogeneous $g, h \in S_d$, write $g/h$ for the
rational function they define, the element $g/h$ of the stalk $S_{((0))}$ at the
generic point. Let $f \in S_1$ be nonzero, so that $D_+(f) \cong \operatorname{Spec} S_{(f)}$
and $K(X) = \operatorname{Frac} S_{(f)}$. Then $g/h$ corresponds to the dehomogenized
fraction $(g/f^d)/(h/f^d)$. Consequently, for every prime divisor $Y$ of $X$
meeting $D_+(f)$, with $\mathfrak{p} \subset S_{(f)}$ the height-one prime of
$Y \cap D_+(f)$,

$v_Y(g/h) = v_{\mathfrak{p}}(g/f^d) - v_{\mathfrak{p}}(h/f^d)$,

where $v_{\mathfrak{p}}$ is the valuation of the discrete valuation ring
$(S_{(f)})_{\mathfrak{p}}$.

Since $S$ is generated by $S_1$, the charts $D_+(f)$ with $f \in S_1$ cover $X$,
so the principal divisor $(g/h)$ is computed chart by chart, and any two
degree-one charts containing $Y$ give the same order.

Example 6.6.1 computes divisor classes on the quadric surface, and Proposition
6.4 treats rational functions on projective space as quotients of homogeneous
forms of equal degree. This article supplies, once and for a graded domain, the
chart description of the divisor of such a quotient; it is used for Example
6.6.1, and it also covers the projective-space case of Proposition 6.4. It is a
project bridge.

## Proof

The rational function $g/h$ is the germ at the generic point of the section
$g/h$ over $D_+(h)$. Restricted to $D_+(fh) = D_+(f) \cap D_+(h)$, that section is
the image of $(g/f^d)/(h/f^d)$ under $S_{(f)} \to (S_{(f)})_{h/f^d} = S_{(fh)}$, so
its germ at the generic point, which lies in every nonempty open, is the stated
fraction of $\operatorname{Frac} S_{(f)}$. The stalk of $X$ at the generic point of $Y$
is the stalk of the open subscheme $D_+(f)$ there, namely $(S_{(f)})_{\mathfrak{p}}$,
compatibly with the maps to $K(X)$. The order along $Y$ is the valuation of this
DVR, and the valuation of a quotient is the difference of the valuations of
numerator and denominator.
