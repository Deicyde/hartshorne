# Ambient hypersurfaces cut divisors

Let $k$ be algebraically closed, let $X \subseteq \mathbb{P}^n_k$ be a closed variety
nonsingular in codimension one, and let $V \subseteq \mathbb{P}^n_k$ be an irreducible
hypersurface not containing $X$. For every codimension-one component $Y_i$ of
$V \cap X$, use a local equation of $V$ to define its multiplicity
$\operatorname{ord} _{Y_i}(f|_X)$, and define the Weil divisor $V\cdot X$ as their weighted sum.

Prove independence of all local equations and extend linearly to ambient
divisors none of whose components contain $X$. This is precisely Exercise
6.2(a); descent to class groups from part (b) is not part of this node.

An irreducible hypersurface of $\mathbb{P}^n_k$ ($n\ge 1$) is the same as a prime divisor
of $\mathbb{P}^n_k$: the homogeneous prime of a codimension-one point is generated by one
irreducible homogeneous polynomial $f$, and conversely $(f)$ is such a prime. A local
equation of $V$ on an open set $U$ is a regular function on $U$ whose germ at each point
$q\in U$ generates the ideal of $V$ in $\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^n,q}$, that is, differs by
a unit from $f/g$ for a homogeneous $g$ of degree $\deg f$ with $g(q)\neq 0$. The
coefficient of $V\cdot X$ along a prime divisor $Y$ of $X$ vanishes unless $Y\subseteq V$,
and every point has a neighbourhood carrying a local equation, for instance $f/x_i^{\deg f}$
on $D_+(x_i)$. The construction and the independence of local equations use neither that
$k$ is algebraically closed nor that $X\to\mathbb{P}^n_k$ is a closed immersion: they hold
for any morphism to $\mathbb{P}^n_k$ from a scheme satisfying $(*)$.
