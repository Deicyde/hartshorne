# Homogeneous ideals

A graded ring is a ring `S` with a decomposition `S = ⨁_{d ≥ 0} S_d` into
abelian groups satisfying `S_d · S_e ⊆ S_{d+e}`. An ideal `𝔞 ⊆ S` is
*homogeneous* when `𝔞 = ⨁_d (𝔞 ∩ S_d)`. Hartshorne quotes without proof that an
ideal is homogeneous iff generated by homogeneous elements, that sums, products,
intersections and radicals of homogeneous ideals are homogeneous, and that
primeness can be tested on homogeneous elements alone. The grading on
`S = k[x₀,…,xₙ]` puts monomials of total degree `d` in `S_d`.

Mathlib covers this well: `GradedRing`, `HomogeneousIdeal`,
`Ideal.IsHomogeneous`, the radical and prime lemmas, and the standard grading on
`MvPolynomial` by total degree. This node is the audit that the pieces
Hartshorne uses are all present and agree with his conventions, not new
mathematics.

One convention to pin down early: Hartshorne grades by `ℕ` starting at `0` and
never uses negative degrees, so the `ℕ`-graded API is the right one, but the
graded localizations in §3 introduce formal degree differences and will need
care.
