# Homogeneous ideals

A graded ring is a ring $S$ with a decomposition $S = \bigoplus _{d \ge 0} S_d$ into
abelian groups satisfying $S_d \cdot S_e \subseteq S_{d+e}$. An ideal $\mathfrak{a} \subseteq S$ is
*homogeneous* when $\mathfrak{a} = \bigoplus _d (\mathfrak{a} \cap S_d)$. Hartshorne quotes without proof that an
ideal is homogeneous iff generated by homogeneous elements, that sums, products,
intersections and radicals of homogeneous ideals are homogeneous, and that
primeness can be tested on homogeneous elements alone. The grading on
$S = k[x_{0},\ldots ,x_{n}]$ puts monomials of total degree $d$ in $S_d$.
