# The homogeneous vanishing ideal

For $Y \subseteq \mathbb{P} ^{n}$, the *homogeneous ideal* $J(Y) \subseteq S$ is the ideal generated by the
homogeneous $f \in S$ vanishing at every point of $Y$. For an algebraic set $Y$
the *homogeneous coordinate ring* is $S(Y) = S/J(Y)$.

$J(Y)$ is homogeneous by construction, and the order-reversing properties
mirroring [Proposition I.1.2](../affine-varieties/vanishing-ideal.md) hold: $T_{1} \subseteq T_{2}$ gives $Z(T_{1}) \supseteq Z(T_{2})$, $Y_{1} \subseteq Y_{2}$ gives
$J(Y_{1}) \supseteq J(Y_{2})$, $J(Y_{1} \cup Y_{2}) = J(Y_{1}) \cap J(Y_{2})$, and $Z(J(Y)) = \overline{Y}$ (Exercise 2.3).

Unlike $A(Y)$, the ring $S(Y)$ is not an invariant of the variety: it depends on
the chosen embedding into projective space. Hartshorne flags this in §3 and it
is why Theorem 3.4 describes $\mathcal{O} _P$ and $K(Y)$ through graded localizations of
$S(Y)$ rather than identifying $S(Y)$ itself with anything intrinsic.
