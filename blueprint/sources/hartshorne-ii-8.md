## Modules and sheaves of differentials

- **Definition and Propositions 8.1A–8.5, pp.172–173.** The module
  `Omega_(B/A)` represents `A`-derivations from an `A`-algebra `B`. It is the
  conormal module `I/I^2` of the multiplication map `B tensor_A B -> B`, is
  compatible with base change and localization, and has the two standard exact
  sequences for a tower and a quotient. Polynomial differentials are free on
  the coordinate differentials, and finite-type algebras have finite
  differential modules.
- **Theorem 8.6A, Proposition 8.7, Theorem 8.8, and Lemma 8.9,
  pp.174–175.** For a finitely generated field extension, the dimension of
  differentials is at least the transcendence degree, with equality exactly
  for a separably generated extension. At a local `k`-algebra with residue
  field `k`, the cotangent space is the fibre of the differential module. Over
  a perfect field, a finite-type local algebra is regular exactly when its
  differential module is free of the expected rank. Lemma 8.9 supplies the
  finite-module freeness criterion used in that proof.
- **Definition and Remarks 8.9.1–8.9.2, pp.175–176.** For a scheme morphism
  `X -> Y`, define `Omega_(X/Y)` as the conormal sheaf of the diagonal. On an
  affine chart `Spec B -> Spec A` it is the sheaf associated to
  `Omega_(B/A)`. It is quasi-coherent, and coherent for a finite-type morphism
  over a Noetherian base.
- **Propositions 8.10–8.12 and Theorem 8.13, pp.175–177.** Relative
  differentials commute with base change, satisfy the transitivity and closed-
  immersion conormal sequences, and on projective space fit into the Euler
  sequence

  `0 -> Omega_(P^n_A/A) -> O(-1)^(n+1) -> O -> 0`.

## Nonsingular varieties and Bertini

- **Definition and Theorem 8.14A, p.177.** An abstract variety is
  nonsingular when all its local rings are regular local rings. This agrees
  with the Chapter-I closed-point definition because every prime localization
  of a regular local ring is regular.
- **Theorem 8.15 and Corollary 8.16, pp.177–178.** For an irreducible
  separated finite-type scheme over an algebraically closed field,
  `Omega_(X/k)` is locally free of rank `dim X` exactly when `X` is
  nonsingular. Every variety has a dense nonsingular open subset.
- **Theorem 8.17, pp.178–179.** If `Y` is an irreducible closed subscheme of
  a nonsingular variety `X`, then `Y` is nonsingular exactly when its
  differential sheaf is locally free and its conormal sequence is also exact
  on the left. In that case its ideal is locally generated by
  `codim(Y,X)` elements and its conormal sheaf is locally free of that rank.
- **Theorem 8.18 and Remark 8.18.1, pp.179–180.** The bad-hyperplane
  incidence correspondence has dimension one less than the complete linear
  system. Its proper image is therefore a proper closed subset, so regular
  hyperplane sections of a nonsingular projective variety form a dense open
  set. The same conclusion holds when the variety has finitely many singular
  points, after avoiding hyperplanes through those points. The theorem asserts
  regularity, not connectedness or irreducibility; those are supplied later in
  III.7.9.1.

The proof treats `X = P^n` separately. In the remaining case `dim X < n`, the
bad hyperplanes at a point form `P^(n-r-1)`. The local input is the regular-
local criterion that, for a regular local ring and a nonzero hypersurface
equation in its maximal ideal, the quotient is regular exactly when the
equation is not in the square of the maximal ideal.

The field is algebraically closed here. The roadmap does not silently replace
Hartshorne's nonsingularity by scheme smoothness over an arbitrary field: over
an imperfect field those notions differ. No later generic-smoothness theorem
from Chapter III is a prerequisite of this DAG.

## Canonical sheaves and adjunction

- **Definitions, pp.180–181.** For a nonsingular `n`-fold, define the tangent
  sheaf as the dual of `Omega_(X/k)`, the canonical sheaf as
  `omega_X = exterior^n Omega_(X/k)`, and, in the projective case, the
  geometric genus as `p_g = dim_k Gamma(X, omega_X)`.
- **Theorem 8.19, pp.181–182.** The geometric genus of a nonsingular
  projective variety is a birational invariant. Properness extends a rational
  map across codimension-one points, and normality extends canonical sections
  across the remaining codimension-at-least-two complement.
- **Normal and conormal sheaves and Proposition 8.20, p.182.** For a
  nonsingular subvariety `Y` of `X`, dualizing the conormal sequence gives the
  tangent-normal sequence. Taking determinants gives

  `omega_Y ~= omega_X|_Y tensor det(N_(Y/X))`.

  For a nonsingular effective Cartier divisor with associated invertible sheaf
  `L = O_X(Y)`, this becomes `omega_Y ~= (omega_X tensor L)|_Y`.
- **Examples 8.20.1–8.20.3, pp.182–184.** The Euler sequence gives
  `omega_(P^n) ~= O(-n-1)`. Bertini produces smooth hypersurfaces of every
  positive degree, and adjunction gives
  `omega_Y ~= O_Y(d-n-1)` for a smooth degree-`d` hypersurface. The resulting
  positive geometric genera obstruct rationality.

The names "K3 surface" and "surface of general type" in Example 8.20.3 are
historical terminology, not theorem targets. The cited nonrationality of a
general cubic or quartic threefold uses methods outside this roadmap and is
out of scope. The claim that cubic surfaces are rational is also only a later
Chapter-V pointer here. None of these comments becomes an II.8 leaf.

An invertible canonical sheaf determines a Cartier divisor only up to linear
equivalence; there is no canonical choice of a divisor representative. The
roadmap records the canonical divisor *class* and does not assert a preferred
`K_X`.

## Local algebra and local complete intersections

- **Theorem 8.21A, pp.184–185.** Regular local rings are Cohen–Macaulay;
  Cohen–Macaulayness localizes; a sequence in the maximal ideal is regular
  exactly when it cuts dimension by its length; quotients by regular sequences
  remain Cohen–Macaulay; and the associated graded algebra of an ideal
  generated by a regular sequence is the symmetric algebra of `I/I^2`.
- **Theorem 8.22A, p.185.** Serre's criterion characterizes a Noetherian
  normal ring by conditions `(R1)` and `(S2)`.
- **Definition, Example 8.22.1, Remark 8.22.2, and Proposition 8.23,
  pp.185–186.** A closed subscheme is a local complete intersection in a
  nonsingular ambient variety when its ideal is locally generated by its
  codimension. Such a subscheme is Cohen–Macaulay and is normal exactly when
  it is regular in codimension one. Hartshorne says that independence from the
  chosen nonsingular embedding follows from the cotangent complex, but gives
  no proof and does not use the assertion later. It remains a source-blocked
  note, not a roadmap leaf.
- **Theorem 8.24, pp.186–187.** Blowing up a nonsingular variety along a
  nonsingular center is nonsingular. Its exceptional divisor is canonically
  `P(J/J^2)`, and its normal sheaf is the relative `O(-1)`. With Hartshorne's
  Rees grading, the inverse-image ideal is `O(1) = O(-E)`, so restricting and
  dualizing gives `N_(E/Bl_Y X) = O_E(-1)`.
- **Theorem 8.25A, p.187.** A complete local ring containing `k`, whose
  residue field is separably generated over `k`, has a field of
  representatives containing `k`.

Hartshorne's suffix `A` marks background results quoted without proof. The
roadmap records Theorems 8.21A, 8.22A, and 8.25A with `origin: background`, as
it does the earlier algebraic `A`-results. They remain source-grounded
prerequisites rather than claims proved in the running text.

## Adopted exercises and later-use evidence

- **Exercise 8.2, p.187.** A globally generated locally free sheaf of rank
  greater than the dimension has a nowhere-vanishing section and a locally
  free quotient. V Exercise 2.3(a) uses this to construct a filtration of a
  vector bundle on a curve with invertible successive quotients.
- **Exercise 8.3, pp.187–188.** Product differentials split as the sum of
  the two pullbacks, canonical sheaves tensor accordingly, and the product of
  a smooth plane cubic with itself has `p_g = 1` but `p_a = -1`. The canonical
  product formula is cited in Example 8.20.3 and again in Chapters IV and V.
- **Exercise 8.4(a–g), p.188.** Global complete intersections are
  scheme-theoretic intersections of hypersurfaces; normal complete
  intersections are projectively normal and have surjective restriction maps;
  iterative Bertini produces smooth complete intersections; adjunction
  computes their canonical sheaves and geometric genera. Chapters III and IV
  repeatedly cite these results.
- **Exercise 8.6, pp.188–189.** A finitely generated algebra whose affine
  scheme is nonsingular has the square-zero infinitesimal lifting property.
  Later deformation exercises cite this construction.
- **Exercise 8.7, pp.189–190.** Every coherent infinitesimal extension of an
  affine nonsingular scheme is trivial. Its conclusion on p.190 is included by
  the explicit exercise exception and is used in III.4 and III.9.
- **Exercise 8.8, p.190.** Plurigenera and the dimensions of spaces of
  regular `q`-forms are birational invariants. Chapter V cites the latter.

Exercise 8.5 is intentionally excluded: its later mentions are alternate
proofs or see-also references after independent results, not dependencies.
Exercise 8.1 is also excluded because its strengthened nonclosed-point
statements are not subsequently cited.
