# Hartshorne Appendix A, overview and sections 1–2

The primary source is the repository PDF, *Algebraic Geometry* (1977),
Appendix A, overview and §§1–2, printed pp.424–429. Section 2 ends with the
projective-bundle formula A11 on p.429. The Chern-class definition beginning
immediately afterward belongs to §3 and is outside this source unit.

The base field is algebraically closed. The main intersection theorem is for
nonsingular quasi-projective varieties and has no characteristic restriction.
Hartshorne explicitly describes the appendix as an outline: the definitions
are source specifications, but the existence theorem and several properties
are not supplied with complete proofs.

## Overview, printed pp.424–425

The curve and surface Riemann–Roch formulas motivate a general intersection
theory in which divisor intersections are higher-codimension cycles. Rational
equivalence generalizes linear equivalence, Chern classes replace a divisor
class by the collection attached to a vector bundle, and generalized
Riemann–Roch uses Chern classes of both the bundle and the tangent sheaf. The
Chern and Riemann–Roch claims are forward context for later Appendix sections,
not results of the present source unit.

## Cycles and rational equivalence, printed pp.425–426

A codimension-`r` cycle is a finite integral linear combination of irreducible
closed codimension-`r` subvarieties. For a codimension-`r` closed subscheme,
its associated cycle uses the codimension-`r` irreducible components with
coefficients equal to their generic local lengths.

For an integral subvariety `Y` and a morphism `f`, raw pushforward is zero if
dimension drops; otherwise its coefficient is the finite function-field
degree over the closure of the image. This raw operation must be distinguished
from the proper pushforward on Chow groups in A3.

Rational equivalence is generated by pushing linearly equivalent Weil
divisors from the normalizations of all subvarieties. On a normal variety its
codimension-one specialization is Weil-divisor linear equivalence. Define

`A^r(X)=Z^r(X)/Rat^r(X)` and `A(X)=direct-sum_r A^r(X)`.

For integral `X`, `A^0(X)=Z`; groups above the dimension vanish. If `X` is
complete of dimension `n`, summing coefficients gives a degree map
`A^n(X)->Z`, well-defined because principal divisors on complete curves have
degree zero.

## Intersection-theory axioms, printed pp.426–427

For a class of varieties closed under products, an intersection theory gives
products `A^r(X) times A^s(X) -> A^(r+s)(X)`. For `f:X->X'`, the graph formula
is

`f^*(Y')=p1_*(Gamma_f . p2^(-1)(Y'))`.

The inverse image is under the second projection; any OCR reading with
`p1^(-1)(Y')` is type-incorrect.

- A1: the product makes `A(X)` a commutative associative graded ring with
  identity.
- A2: arbitrary pullback is a functorial ring homomorphism.
- A3: proper pushforward is a functorial graded-group homomorphism, with the
  appropriate degree shift.
- A4: `f_*(x.f^*y)=f_*x.y`.
- A5: `Y.Z=Delta^*(Y times Z)`.
- A6: a proper intersection is the sum of its components with local integer
  multiplicities.
- A7: intersection with an effective Cartier divisor is its restricted
  fundamental cycle; smooth transverse intersections have multiplicity one.

Theorem 1.1 asserts a unique such theory on nonsingular quasi-projective
varieties. Hartshorne only outlines the proof. Serre's local multiplicity is
the alternating finite-length Tor Euler characteristic. Chow's moving lemma
makes cycles meet properly and asserts independence of the moved
representative. Uniqueness proceeds by moving, diagonal reduction, the local
complete-intersection property of the diagonal, and repeated Cartier
normalization.

## Examples, printed p.428

Example 1.1.1 shows why higher Tor terms are necessary: the union of two
coordinate planes meeting at the origin has geometric intersection
multiplicity two with the diagonal plane, while the naive tensor-product
quotient has length three.

Example 1.1.2 shows that the integral intersection theory of Theorem 1.1
cannot simply be extended to every singular variety. On the quadric cone,
linearity and `2M` being a hyperplane section would force `L.M=1/2`. This
does not exclude operational Chow theory or other singular theories.

## Properties of the Chow ring, printed pp.428–429

- A8: `A^1(X) ~= Pic(X)` for smooth quasi-projective `X`.
- On a smooth projective surface, the product on `A^1` followed by degree
  recovers the V.1 intersection pairing.
- A9: pullback along `X times A^m -> X` is an isomorphism.
- A10: for a nonsingular closed subvariety `Y` with complement `U`,
  `A(Y) -> A(X) -> A(U) -> 0` is exact; no injectivity on the left is claimed.
- `A(P^n)=Z[h]/(h^(n+1))`.
- A11: for rank-`r` locally free `E`, `A(P(E))` is free over `A(X)` on
  `1,xi,...,xi^(r-1)`, where `xi=[O(1)]` in Hartshorne's quotient convention.

Hartshorne sends A9–A11 to Chevalley for proofs.

## Proof sources and blockers

The explicit source roots are:

1. proper pushforward preserving rational equivalence, including A.6.2;
2. Serre's Tor intersection multiplicity and all required properties;
3. Chow's moving lemma and independence of the moved representative;
4. existence of the A1–A7 product;
5. affine homotopy invariance;
6. localization exactness;
7. the projective-bundle formula.

Exact passages must be adopted from Serre, Chevalley, Roberts, Fulton, or the
Stacks Project. Hartshorne's outline and generic bibliography are not complete
proof specifications.

## Pinned Mathlib audit

Pinned Mathlib contains `AlgebraicGeometry.AlgebraicCycle` and a weighted
pushforward for quasi-compact morphisms. It has no rational-equivalence
quotient, Chow group or ring, moving lemma, Serre intersection multiplicity,
localization theorem, or projective-bundle formula. The existing cycle API is
infrastructure only; no article below is an exact Mathlib leaf.

## Representation warnings

- Use cohomological grading by codimension; record the shift under proper
  pushforward.
- Mathlib cycles use generic points and locally finite support, whereas a
  fixed-codimension cycle on a Noetherian variety is a finite sum.
- Keep raw cycle pushforward separate from its descent through rational
  equivalence.
- The degree formula shown assumes the algebraically closed base; a general
  base would require residue degrees.
- Pullback for arbitrary morphisms is part of the smooth intersection theory,
  not naive scheme-theoretic inverse image.
- The Chow ring is ordinarily commutative in its grading, with no Koszul sign.
- Preserve the quotient convention for `P(E)` and `O(1)`.
