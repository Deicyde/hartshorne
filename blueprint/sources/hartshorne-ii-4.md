# Hartshorne II.4, *Separated and Proper Morphisms*

Robin Hartshorne, *Algebraic Geometry*, Chapter II, §4. Locators are printed
book pages. In the repository scan, `PDF index = printed page + 14` when the
first page has index zero.

## Source boundary

The section starts on printed p. 95. Its running text ends on p. 105 after
Theorem 4.11A, and the exercises continue through p. 108. The approved source
span had ended at p. 107, but Exercise 4.11 begins there and finishes on p. 108;

## Separatedness and the valuative criterion

- **Definition, p. 96.** For `f : X → Y`, the diagonal is the unique map
  `X → X ×_Y X` whose two projections are the identity. The morphism is
  separated when this diagonal is a closed immersion. A scheme is separated
  when its structure morphism to `Spec ℤ` is separated.
- **Proposition 4.1 and Corollary 4.2, pp. 96–97.** Every morphism of affine
  schemes is separated. More generally, separatedness is equivalent to the
  diagonal having closed image; the remaining immersion data of the diagonal
  is automatic locally on affine charts.
- **Theorem 4.3, pp. 97–99.** If `X` is Noetherian, `f : X → Y` is separated
  exactly when every square `Spec K → X`, `Spec R → Y`, for a valuation
  ring `R` with fraction field `K`, has at most one lift `Spec R → X`.
- **Lemma 4.4, pp. 97–98.** Maps from `Spec K` are points with residue-field
  embeddings. Maps from `Spec R` are described by a generic image, a
  specialization, and domination by `R` of the local ring on the reduced
  closure of the generic image.
- **Lemma 4.5, p. 98.** The image of a quasi-compact scheme morphism is closed
  exactly when it is stable under specialization.
- **Corollary 4.6, pp. 99–100.** Open and closed immersions are separated;
  separatedness is stable under composition, base change, and products; it
  cancels from a composite; and it is local on the target.

Mathlib states the valuative theorem without Hartshorne's excess Noetherian
hypothesis: separatedness is uniqueness of valuation lifts together with
quasi-separatedness. A Noetherian source supplies the latter. Roadmap nodes
should expose this exact upstream theorem and record Hartshorne's statement as
its immediate specialization.

## Properness and the valuative criterion

- **Definition, p. 100.** A morphism is universally closed when every base
  change is a closed map. It is proper when separated, finite type, and
  universally closed.
- **Theorem 4.7, pp. 101–102.** For a finite-type morphism with Noetherian
  source, properness is equivalent to existence and uniqueness of a lift in
  every valuation square.
- **Corollary 4.8, pp. 102–103.** Closed immersions are proper; properness is
  stable under composition, base change, and products; it cancels against a
  separated second map; and it is local on the target.

Mathlib decomposes finite type as local finite type plus quasi-compactness.
Its exact valuative theorem says that properness is the conjunction of the
unique-lift criterion, quasi-compactness, quasi-separatedness, and local finite
type. Universal closedness itself is quasi-compactness plus the existence
criterion.

## Projective morphisms

- **Definitions and Example 4.8.1, p. 103.** Define
  `ℙⁿ_Y = ℙⁿ_ℤ × Y`. A projective morphism factors as a closed
  immersion into some `ℙⁿ_Y` followed by projection. A quasi-projective
  morphism factors as an open immersion followed by a projective morphism. If
  a graded `A`-algebra is generated by its degree-one part, its `Proj` is
  projective over `Spec A`.
- **Theorem 4.9, pp. 103–104.** A projective morphism of Noetherian schemes is
  proper. A quasi-projective morphism of Noetherian schemes is finite type and
  separated. The proof reduces to projective space and extends a generic
  valuation point by choosing a coordinate of minimal valuation.

Pinned Mathlib already proves `Proj → Spec A₀` proper for finite-type graded
algebras. It does not package relative projective space over an arbitrary
scheme, projective morphisms, or a relative Segre embedding.

## Varieties and integral closure

- **Proposition 4.10, pp. 104–105.** For algebraically closed `k`, the essential
  image of Hartshorne's variety-to-scheme functor is the quasi-projective
  integral schemes over `k`; projective varieties correspond to projective
  integral schemes.
- **Definitions, p. 105.** An abstract variety is an integral separated scheme
  of finite type over an algebraically closed field. It is complete when it is
  proper over the field.
- **Theorem 4.11A, p. 105.** The integral closure of a subring `A` of a field
  `K` is the intersection of all valuation subrings of `K` containing `A`.
  Hartshorne cites Bourbaki, Ch. VI, §1, no. 3, Theorem 3, p. 92. Pinned
  Mathlib contains this theorem as `iInf_valuationSubring_superset`.

The historical remarks about complete nonprojective varieties are context, not
additional formalization targets.

## Adopted exercises

- **Exercise 4.3, p. 106:** affine opens of a separated scheme have affine
  intersection. Used on II p. 115 and III pp. 222 and 252.
- **Exercise 4.4, p. 106:** the scheme-theoretic image of a proper `S`-scheme
  in a separated finite-type `S`-scheme is closed and proper over `S`. Used on
  II pp. 120, 137, and 152.
- **Exercise 4.5(a),(b), p. 106:** a valuation has at most one center on a
  separated integral scheme and exactly one center on a proper integral
  scheme. Used on II pp. 131 and 137.
- **Exercise 4.6, p. 106:** a proper morphism of affine varieties is finite.
  Theorem 4.11A explicitly delegates its application to this exercise.
- **Exercise 4.9, p. 107, and Exercise 4.8(e):** projective morphisms compose,
  and projectivity cancels from `g ∘ f` to `f` when `g` is separated. These
  are the later-used composition/calculus clauses, invoked on II p. 166 and
  III p. 280.
- **Exercise 4.11, pp. 107–108:** for finite-type morphisms of Noetherian
  schemes, the valuative criteria may be checked on DVRs. Used on IV p. 325.

Exercise 4.11(a), read literally for a field local domain, has a false edge
case: a field admits no dominating nonfield DVR with the same fraction field.
The geometric reduction uses the nontrivial-specialization case, equivalently
a nonzero maximal ideal in the local domain produced from the specialization.
The roadmap therefore states the repaired lemma with that hypothesis and uses
the trivial specialization separately in the DVR criterion. This repair is
project-authored and is not attributed verbatim to Hartshorne. In its local
blow-up step, choose generators `x₁,…,xₙ` of the maximal ideal with
`x₁ ≠ 0`; the argument does not assume that the maximal ideal is principal.

## Excluded exercises and examples

Exercises 4.1–4.2, 4.7, 4.10, and 4.12 are not adopted: no later running-text
dependency in the approved scope was found. Exercise 4.5(c) is a starred
converse and 4.5(d) is the global-functions consequence; neither is needed by
the later citation. Of Exercise 4.8, only cancellation clause (e) is adopted
through its later use with Exercise 4.9. Clauses (a)–(d) remain proof calculus,
not independent exercise targets, and clause (f), concerning reduction of a
morphism, is excluded because no later running-text use was found. The
doubled-origin and affine-line examples remain prospective tests rather than
theorem nodes.

## Representation and upstream gaps

Use Mathlib's `ValuativeCommSq`, in which `R → K` is an algebra satisfying
`IsFractionRing`, instead of requiring a literal subring inclusion. Define a
valuation center first by a lift of the canonical generic-point map; prove its
equivalence with domination of the local ring as a supporting theorem. This
avoids choosing incompatible embeddings of every stalk into the function
field.

Substantive gaps are: Hartshorne's exact two-point formulation of Lemma 4.4;
relative projective spaces and projective morphisms; the scheme-level relative
Segre closed immersion; a project-local essential-image theorem for the
existing variety functor; and the DVR-only valuative criterion. The last is
explicitly listed as future work in pinned
`Mathlib/AlgebraicGeometry/ValuativeCriterion.lean`, and no open Mathlib pull
request for it was found.
