## Weil divisors and class groups

- **Definitions, p.130.** A scheme is regular in codimension one when its
  one-dimensional local rings are regular. Throughout the Weil-divisor part,
  condition `(*)` means Noetherian, integral, separated, and regular in
  codimension one. Prime divisors are integral closed subschemes of codimension
  one; Weil divisors are finite integral linear combinations of them, and an
  effective divisor has nonnegative coefficients. The codimension-one local
  ring is a DVR whose valuation is denoted `v_Y`.
- **Lemma 6.1 and definitions, p.131.** A nonzero rational function has
  nonzero order along only finitely many prime divisors. This defines its
  principal divisor `(f)`, linear equivalence, and the divisor class group
  `Cl X`.
- **Proposition 6.2 and Proposition 6.3A, pp.131–132.** A Noetherian domain
  `A` is a UFD iff `Spec A` is normal and has trivial divisor class group. The
  proof uses the height-one intersection theorem
  `A = intersection_(ht p=1) A_p` for an integrally closed Noetherian domain.
- **Proposition 6.4, pp.132–133.** Degree identifies `Cl(P^n_k)` with `Z`,
  generated by a hyperplane.
- **Proposition 6.5, p.133.** Restriction to an open complement is surjective
  on class groups, is an isomorphism after removing codimension at least two,
  and has the stated exact sequence for an irreducible codimension-one
  complement.
- **Proposition 6.6, pp.134–135.** Pullback along `X × A^1 → X` identifies
  the two class groups.
- **Examples 6.6.1–6.6.2, pp.135–136.** The nonsingular quadric surface has
  class group `Z + Z`; its two rulings have types `(1,0)` and `(0,1)`, and its
  hyperplane class has type `(1,1)`. Later running text repeatedly uses these
  identifications, so they are a roadmap target.

The affine-space, Dedekind-domain, plane-complement, affine-quadric-cone, and
twisted-cubic calculations are retained as examples or tests. The statement
about the class group of a cubic surface is a preview of V.4.8 and is deferred
there.

## Divisors on curves

- **Proposition 6.7, pp.136–137.** For a nonsingular curve over an
  algebraically closed field, projective, complete, and arising from the
  abstract nonsingular curve of I.6 are equivalent.
- **Proposition 6.8, p.137.** A morphism from a complete nonsingular curve to
  a curve is constant or surjective. In the surjective case it is finite, the
  induced function-field extension is finite, and the target is complete.
- **Definitions, pp.137–138.** The degree of a finite curve morphism is its
  function-field degree. Divisors on a nonsingular curve are sums of closed
  points. For a finite morphism `f : X → Y`, pullback is defined by
  `f*Q = sum_(P maps to Q) v_P(t) P`; it is independent of the local parameter
  and preserves linear equivalence.
- **Proposition 6.9, p.138.** `deg(f*D) = deg(f) deg(D)`.
- **Corollary 6.10, p.138.** Principal divisors on complete nonsingular curves
  have degree zero, and degree gives a surjection `Cl X → Z`.
- **Examples 6.10.1–6.10.2, pp.138–140.** A complete nonsingular curve is
  rational iff two distinct points are linearly equivalent. For the stated
  nonsingular plane cubic, `P ↦ [P-P_0]` identifies the closed points with
  `Cl^0 X` and gives the chord-and-tangent group law. The proof uses Exercise
  I.6.2(e), which establishes that the affine cubic `y^2=x^3-x` is not
  rational. Both curve conclusions are used later.

Remark 6.10.3's general Jacobian, Picard-variety, and Néron–Severi assertions
are not proved in the book and remain external source obligations rather than
underspecified formalization nodes.

## Cartier divisors and invertible sheaves

- **Definitions, p.141.** For an arbitrary scheme, the total quotient rings on
  affine opens sheafify to `K_X`. A Cartier divisor is a global section of
  `K_X^* / O_X^*`; principal divisors, linear equivalence, and `CaCl X` are
  defined without reducedness or integrality hypotheses.
- **Proposition 6.11 and Remarks 6.11.1A–6.11.2, pp.141–142.** On an integral
  separated Noetherian locally factorial scheme, Weil and Cartier divisors
  agree, compatibly with principal divisors. More generally, on a normal
  scheme Cartier divisors are the locally principal Weil divisors. Regular
  local rings are UFDs, so regular schemes under these hypotheses are locally
  factorial.
- **Proposition 6.12, p.143.** Tensor product and duals make the isomorphism
  classes of invertible sheaves into `Pic X`.
- **Proposition 6.13 and Corollary 6.14, p.144.** A Cartier divisor determines
  an invertible subsheaf `O_X(D)` of `K_X`; this identifies Cartier divisors
  with invertible subsheaves of `K_X`, respects subtraction/tensor product,
  and identifies linear equivalence with abstract sheaf isomorphism. Hence
  `CaCl X → Pic X` is injective.
- **Proposition 6.15, p.145.** For an integral scheme, every invertible sheaf
  embeds in the constant function-field sheaf, so `CaCl X → Pic X` is an
  isomorphism.
- **Corollaries 6.16–6.17, p.145.** For an integral separated Noetherian
  locally factorial scheme, `Cl X ≅ Pic X`; every invertible sheaf on
  projective space is `O(l)` for a unique integer `l`.
- **Definition, Remark 6.17.1, and Proposition 6.18, pp.145–146.** Effective
  Cartier divisors correspond to locally principal closed subschemes cut out
  locally by non-zero-divisors, and the associated ideal sheaf is `O_X(-D)`.

The affine quadric cone and cuspidal cubic are examples, not independent
targets. The latter's identification with the additive group uses an
elementary calculation left to the reader and is not needed by later running
text.

## Adopted exercises and later-use evidence

- **Exercise 6.2 preamble, p.146.** For a projective variety nonsingular in
  codimension one, define the degree of a Weil divisor by weighting each prime
  divisor by its projective degree. This is the codomain of the intersection
  calculation below.
- **Exercise 6.2(a), p.146.** Construct the divisor cut on a projective
  variety by an ambient hypersurface, with valuation multiplicities. This is
  prerequisite data for the later-used degree calculation.
- **Exercise 6.2(c), p.146.** Generalized Bezout gives
  `deg(V.X) = deg(V) deg(X)`. IV.3.3.2 uses the resulting equality between the
  degree of a very ample divisor and the degree of its image.
- **Exercise 6.4, p.147.** If `char k != 2` and `f` is squarefree, the
  quadratic hypersurface algebra `k[x_1,...,x_n,z]/(z^2-f)` is normal. IV
  Exercise 2.2(b) uses this to construct the nonsingular double cover with six
  prescribed branch points.
- **Exercise 6.8(a), p.148.** Pullback of invertible sheaves induces
  `f* : Pic Y → Pic X`.
- **Exercise 6.8(b), p.148.** For a finite morphism of nonsingular curves,
  `f*O_Y(D) ≅ O_X(f*D)`. IV.2 uses this compatibility explicitly.
- **Exercise 6.10, definition only, p.148.** Define the Grothendieck group
  `K(X)` of coherent sheaves by exact-sequence relations. Later chapters use
  the definition; its calculations and localization sequence are not adopted
  here.
- **Exercise 6.11(b), p.149.** On a nonsingular curve, finite locally free
  resolutions define `det : K(X) → Pic X`, independently of the resolution.
  Later curve and surface arguments use this determinant construction.
- **Exercise 6.12, p.149.** Define the unique additive degree of coherent
  sheaves on a complete nonsingular curve, agreeing with divisor degree on
  `O(D)` and with the sum of stalk lengths on torsion sheaves. V.2 uses this
  to define the degree of a vector bundle.

### Adopted Chapter I exercise prerequisite

- **Exercise I.6.2(a–e), p.46.** For the affine cubic
  `Y : y^2=x^3-x` in characteristic not two, prove nonsingularity and
  normality of its coordinate ring, compute its units using the involution
  `y ↦ -y`, show that the coordinate ring is not a UFD, and conclude that
  `Y` is not rational. Only this exercise from the Chapter I §6 exercise block
  is adopted here, as the nonrationality input to Example II.6.10.2.

  Hartshorne's cited sources or another proof-level reference before planning.
