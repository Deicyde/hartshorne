# Modules and quasi-coherent sheaves

## Prior implementation guidance

This chapter covers the foundational half of Hartshorne II.5 through
Proposition 5.8 for schemes. It ends where ideal sheaves begin on printed p. 115.
The roadmap explicitly relates Mathlib's local-presentation definition of
quasi-coherence to Hartshorne's local affine `M̃` definition before using
one in place of the other.

## Ringed-space generalization plan

Decision (project owner): the module-sheaf layer of II.5 (sheaf Hom, locally free and
invertible sheaves, finite-locally-free duality) and everything stated for a ringed space on
top of it (II Ex. 5.1(d), II Prop. 6.12, III.6 Lemma 6.6, Prop. 6.7, the left-exact sheaf Hom)
gets Lean that holds for an arbitrary ringed space. Scheme statements become specializations.

### Uniform setting

- `{X : TopCat.{u}} {R : Sheaf (Opens.grothendieckTopology X) CommRingCat.{u}}`, modules
  `SheafOfModules.{u} (ringCatSheafOf R)`; `Hartshorne.ringCatSheafOf` is the existing
  abbreviation of `Hartshorne.Scheme.ModuleSheafTensor`, already used by
  `Hartshorne.moduleSheafTensor`.
- Why commutative: the `𝒪_X(U)`-module structure on local Hom and the tensor product over
  `𝒪_X(U)` need commutative section rings (Hartshorne's rings are commutative). Mathlib's
  `RingedSpace`, `LocallyRingedSpace` and `Scheme` all carry a `CommRingCat` sheaf, so
  `R := X.sheaf` specializes, and `SheafOfModules (ringCatSheafOf X.sheaf)` is definitionally
  `X.Modules` (`X.ringCatSheaf` is `(sheafCompose _ (forget₂ CommRingCat RingCat)).obj X.sheaf`).
- Results that need no commutativity stay over `R : Sheaf (Opens.grothendieckTopology X) RingCat`
  (open-restriction-injective, enough injectives, injective-implies-flasque, sections
  representable, Ext¹ classification); they are already general and specialize through
  `ringCatSheafOf R` unchanged.
- Restriction `F|_U` is Mathlib's over-site restriction `SheafOfModules.over F U` over
  `(ringCatSheafOf R).over U` (sections over `V ≤ U` are `F(V)`); Mathlib's
  `TopologicalSpace.Opens.sheafOfModulesEquivOver` identifies it with modules on the subspace
  `U`, and `Scheme.Modules.overEquiv`, `Scheme.Modules.overFunctorEquiv` identify it with
  `F.restrict U.ι` on schemes.
- Morphisms of ringed spaces (projection formula): `f : X ⟶ Y` in `TopCat` and
  `φ : ringCatSheafOf S ⟶ ((Opens.map f).sheafPushforwardContinuous _ _ _).obj (ringCatSheafOf R)`,
  with Mathlib's `SheafOfModules.pushforward φ`, `SheafOfModules.pullback φ`,
  `SheafOfModules.pullbackPushforwardAdjunction φ`. Schemes: `φ := f.toRingCatSheafHom`.
- Namespace `Hartshorne.Ringed` for every new general declaration (inside it, short names
  resolve to the general versions first); new files under `src/Hartshorne/RingedSpace/`, each
  imported by the library root.

### Route: expand, migrate, contract (deprecation deferred to the contract stage)

Each expand stage adds the general declarations in a new file and adds them to the article's
`lean:` beside the existing scheme declarations, which stay unchanged and recorded as the scheme
specialization. No existing declaration changes, so `work impact` has nothing to report and the
claim set is the article itself. Deprecating the scheme declarations at expand time (the strict
default route) would make finite-locally-free-duality statement-impacted and require Agent Review
or retraction of finite-locally-free-duality and Lemma 6.6 although their mathematics does not
change; deferring it to stage 7, after the dependents have their own general Lean, avoids that.
The scheme predicates of locally-free-and-invertible (`Scheme.Modules.IsLocallyFree`,
`IsLocallyFreeOfRank`, `IsInvertible`, `rankAt`) are never deprecated: the scheme articles
coherent-free-locus, coherent-self-flat-iff-locally-free and
locally-free-sections-extend-codimension-two state scheme results with them (deprecating them
makes all three statement-impacted).

### Lean pitfalls seen in the scheme work

- `X.sheaf` is a `TopCat.Sheaf` while `moduleSheafTensor` takes a `Sheaf`; `X.Modules` has its
  own `Category` instance, so annotate `(C := X.Modules)` or use term-mode proofs when a scheme
  user applies a general declaration.
- Instantiating generic duality lemmas at nested duals (`moduleSheafDual (moduleSheafDual A)`)
  followed by `presheaf.map` caused a kernel deterministic timeout; state lemmas over a pairing
  `p : A ⟶ moduleSheafDual B` and instantiate only inside proofs.

### Open question for Roadmap

The rank claim of locally-free-and-invertible does not hold for an arbitrary ringed space. On
the connected two-point space `{a, b}` with opens `∅, {a}, X`, take `𝒪(X) = k` and
`𝒪({a}) = 0`. Then `𝒪_X` is invertible, its rank at `b` is 1, and its rank at `a` is
undetermined, since `𝒪_X|_{a} = 0 ≅ 𝒪^n` for every `n`. A general rank theorem therefore needs
nonzero stalks, which holds for locally ringed spaces and schemes; with that hypothesis the proof
goes through stalks (`(𝒪^(I))_x ≅ 𝒪_x^(I)` and invariant basis number). Until Roadmap restates
the rank claim (nonzero stalks, or a separate locally-ringed article), the scheme rank
declarations stay recorded. The duality, Lemma 6.6, Picard and projection-formula stages use
`Ringed.IsFiniteLocallyFree` and need no rank.
