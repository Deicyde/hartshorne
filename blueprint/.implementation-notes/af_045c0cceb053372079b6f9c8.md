# The globally generated morphism is finite

## Status

Statement phase, not started in Lean. No declarations yet.

## Blocker for the faithful statement

The hypothesis $D\cdot C>0$ needs the surface intersection pairing
(Hartshorne V Theorem 1.1, article `divisor-intersection-pairing`), which is
not formalized and is not among this article's dependencies. The proof also
needs that an effective divisor disjoint from an irreducible curve $C$ has
intersection zero with $C$ (Proposition 1.4, `intersection-number-sum-local-lengths`)
and the correspondence between sections of $\mathcal{O}_X(nD)$ and effective
divisors linearly equivalent to $nD$ (II.7.7). No degree of a line bundle on a
singular curve exists either, so the hypothesis cannot be restated through
restricted degrees.

## Available Lean

- Associated morphism: `Hartshorne.existsUnique_morphism_of_isGeneratedBySections`
  (morphism to `projectiveSpaceOverScheme n Y` with `φ^* 𝒪(1) ≅ L`).
- Projective with finite fibres is finite:
  `Hartshorne.Projective.isFinite_of_finite_fibres`,
  `Hartshorne.Projective.isFinite_of_finite_preimage_singleton`.
- Projective calculus: `Hartshorne.Projective.comp` (ProjectiveMorphismCalculus).
- Surface structure: `Hartshorne.Surface` (integral, regular, projective over
  `k`, dimension two); prime divisors are `Scheme.PrimeDivisor` (coheight-one
  points). V.5.4 encodes irreducible curves as `IrreducibleCloseds` of
  topological dimension one (`Hartshorne.contractedCurves`).

## Planned pairing-free building steps

1. Finite step: for the associated morphism of a surface, finite fibres imply
   that the morphism is finite, and so is its factorization through the
   scheme-theoretic image (Mathlib `Scheme.Hom.toImage`-style API). Needs
   projectivity of a morphism of projective $k$-schemes, or properness plus
   `IsFinite.of_isProper_of_locallyQuasiFinite`.
2. Geometric step: an infinite fibre contains an irreducible curve $C$ of the
   surface, and some generating section $s_i$ is nowhere zero on $C$ (the
   preimage of the chart where $x_i\neq 0$ is the nonvanishing locus of $s_i$).
3. Final theorem: blocked on the pairing as above.
