# Regular hyperplane sections

## Status

Candidate (pending review): `Hartshorne.BertiniIncidence.bertini_regular_hyperplane_section`,
in `Hartshorne.Scheme.Differentials.BertiniRegularHyperplaneSection`.

## Setting used by the Lean

Same conventions as the bad-hyperplane incidence article: `X ⊆ ℙⁿ_k` is a closed immersion
`φ : X ⟶ projectiveSpaceOverScheme n (Spec k)` with `X` integral and `X.IsRegular`, `k`
algebraically closed. The complete linear system `|H|` is the dual projective space
`projectiveSpaceScheme k n`; its closed point `H` is the hyperplane `H_a` when `Represents a H`
with `a ≠ 0` (every closed point represents such an `a`, `exists_represents_of_isClosed`).
`H_a ∩ X` is the closed subscheme `(hyperplaneSectionIdeal φ a).subscheme`.

The theorem states: there is `U ⊆ (ℙⁿ_k)^∨` open and dense such that for every closed point `H`
and every `a ≠ 0` represented by `H`, `H ∈ U` iff some point of `X` is off `H_a ∩ X` (`H_a` does
not contain `X`) and `(hyperplaneSectionIdeal φ a).subscheme.IsRegular` (regular at every point,
not only at closed points). No dimension hypothesis: `r = dim X` is obtained inside the proof.

## Proof outline and helpers (same module)

* `forall_not_isBadHyperplaneAt_iff`: `H_a` is good iff it is bad (`IsBadHyperplaneAt`) at no
  closed point. Regularity spreads from closed points to all points of `H_a ∩ X` through
  `isRegularAt_of_specializes` (stalks at generizations are localizations,
  `NormalFamily.isLocalization_stalkSpecializes`, plus Theorem 8.14A,
  `RegularLocal.isRegularLocalRing_localization_atPrime`); `H_a ∩ X` is Jacobson.
* Case `r < n`: `Z` from `bertini_bad_hyperplane_incidence`; `S = p₂(Z)`.
  `isClosed_image_snd` (via `properImage` with the vanishing-ideal subscheme of a closed set and
  `projectiveSpaceOverSchemeProjection_isProper`) gives closedness of images of closed sets;
  `topologicalKrullDim_image_le` / `topologicalKrullDim_le_of_isClosedMap` (chains of irreducible
  closed sets lift along a closed map onto a quasi-sober space) give `dim S ≤ n - 1`;
  `topologicalKrullDim_projectiveSpace` gives `dim (ℙⁿ_k)^∨ = n`, so `S ≠ univ`; `U = Sᶜ` is dense
  since `(ℙⁿ_k)^∨` is irreducible. A closed `H` lies in `S` iff `H_a` is bad at some closed `x`:
  one way via a closed point of `Z ∩ p₂⁻¹(H)` (Jacobson), the other via
  `incidenceAmbient_exists_closedPoint_of_isClosed` (a closed point over `(x, H)`).
* Case `r ≥ n`: `finrank_badHyperplaneSubspace` gives dimension `n - r = 0`, so no hyperplane is
  bad anywhere and `U = univ`.

## Divergence from the printed proof

The book separates `X = ℙⁿ` and handles it by the projective-space case (every hyperplane
section of `ℙⁿ` is `ℙⁿ⁻¹`). The Lean instead uses the pointwise dimension count of the bad
subspace, which is `n - r = 0` when `r ≥ n`. Using the projective-space-case declarations would
need an identification of `X` with `ℙⁿ_k` and of the zero scheme `H_a ∩ X` (in the model
`projectiveSpaceOverScheme n (Spec k)`) with `Proj (k[x]/(h))` (in the model
`projectiveSpaceScheme k n`), neither of which exists in the library. So the Depends on edge to
the projective-space case is not used by the Lean.

The plain-text Proof depends on bullet (a proper closed subset of irreducible projective space
has smaller dimension) is used only in the trivial converse form: a closed set of dimension
`< n` is not all of `(ℙⁿ_k)^∨`.
