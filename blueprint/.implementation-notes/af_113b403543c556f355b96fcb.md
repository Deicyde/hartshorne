# Monoidal transformation at a point

## Lean

Module `Hartshorne.Surface.PointMonoidalTransformation`. Throughout, `S : Hartshorne.Surface k`
and `P : S.Point` (a closed point).

- `Hartshorne.Surface.pointIdeal`: the ideal sheaf `𝔪_P`, the quasi-coherent ideal subsheaf
  corresponding to Mathlib's `Scheme.IdealSheafData.vanishingIdeal` of the closed set `{P}` (the
  reduced induced structure). Coherence: `isCoherent_pointIdeal` (from
  `SerreAffineness.isCoherent_toModules`, every quasi-coherent ideal on a locally Noetherian
  scheme is coherent). Nonzero: `exists_pointIdeal_ne_bot`, using `S.dim_eq` to find a point
  `x ≠ P` (a one-point space has topological Krull dimension `≤ 0`) and an affine open around
  `x` avoiding `P`, on which `𝔪_P` is the unit ideal.
- `Hartshorne.Surface.pointBlowup`, `pointBlowupProjection`: `X̃ = Bl_P X` and `π`, the
  blowing-up of `pointIdeal` (`QuasicoherentIdealSubsheaf.blowup`, `blowupProjection`).
- `Hartshorne.Surface.isIntegral_pointBlowup` (instance) and `pointBlowup_projective`
  (`π` projective and surjective, `π ≫ S.hom` projective): Proposition II.7.16 via
  `blowup_variety` and `isIntegral_blowup`.
- `Hartshorne.Surface.pointBlowupExceptional`: `E` as an effective Cartier divisor on `X̃`, the
  general exceptional divisor `QuasicoherentIdealSubsheaf.exceptionalDivisor` of the blowing-up
  (II.7.13(a)). `idealSheafData_pointBlowupExceptional`: its ideal sheaf is
  `(vanishingIdeal {P}).comap π`, the ideal of `X̃ ×_X P`, so `E = π⁻¹(P)` scheme-theoretically;
  `coe_support_pointBlowupExceptional`: its support is the fibre `π ⁻¹' {P}`.
- `Hartshorne.Surface.pointCompl` (`X - P`) and `pointBlowupExceptionalCompl` (`X̃ - E`, the
  complement of the support of `E`); `pointBlowupExceptionalCompl_eq`: `X̃ - E = π ⁻¹ᵁ (X - P)`.
- `Hartshorne.Surface.pointBlowupIsoOffCenter`: `X̃ - E ≅ X - P`, built from
  `isIso_pointBlowupProjection_restrict` (via `pointCompl_eq_centerCompl` and
  `isIso_blowupProjection_restrict_centerCompl`, II.7.13(b)); `pointBlowupIsoOffCenter_hom_ι`
  says it lies over `X` (it is the restriction of `π`).

## Scope

That `X̃` is again a Chapter-V surface (nonsingular, dimension two) is Proposition 3.1, a later
article; so `X̃` is a bare scheme here, not a `Hartshorne.Surface`, and `E` is a Cartier divisor
on that scheme rather than a `Surface.Curve`. Once `X̃` is packaged as a surface, `E` becomes a
curve through `Surface.weilCartierEquiv`, and its closed subscheme is the subscheme of
`Scheme.CartierDivisor.idealSheafData`.
