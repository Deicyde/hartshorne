# Formal completion as a locally ringed space

## Lean

Module `Hartshorne.Scheme.FormalCompletion`, namespace
`Hartshorne.QuasicoherentIdealSubsheaf`, for `I : QuasicoherentIdealSubsheaf X` on any scheme
`X` (no Noetherian hypothesis; the article keeps Hartshorne's).

- `formalCompletionDiagram I : ℕᵒᵖ ⥤ TopCat.Sheaf CommRingCat (I.infinitesimalNeighborhood 1)`:
  the inverse system `n ↦ 𝒪_{Y_(n+1)}` (indexed from `0`, so it runs through the book's
  `n ≥ 1`), built with `Functor.ofOpSequence` from the transition maps
  `formalCompletionSheafMap n`.
- `formalCompletionSheaf I := limit (formalCompletionDiagram I)`: the structure sheaf
  `lim_{n ≥ 1} (𝒪_X / I^n)|_Y`, a plain sheaf of commutative rings.
- `isUnit_formalCompletionSheaf_iff`: a section is a unit iff its image in the `n = 1` term
  (`𝒪_Y`) is a unit.
- `isLocalRing_stalk_formalCompletionSheaf` (instance): every stalk is a local ring.
- `formalCompletion I : LocallyRingedSpace`, with `formalCompletion_carrier` and
  `formalCompletion_presheaf` (both `rfl`).

## Encoding

- The space `|Y|` is the underlying space of `Y_1 = I.infinitesimalNeighborhood 1`; for a given
  closed immersion `f : Y ⟶ X` with ideal `I`, `infinitesimalNeighborhoodOneIso f` identifies
  `Y_1` with `Y`.
- `infinitesimalNeighborhoodFromOne n : Y_1 ⟶ Y_(n+1)` is `IdealSheafData.inclusion`; it is
  surjective because `I^(n+1)` and `I` have the same support (Mathlib's `support_pow_succ`,
  reached through `quasicoherentIdealSubsheafEquiv_pow`, i.e. the module-ideal-sheaf /
  affine-ideal correspondence), and a closed embedding, so an open embedding
  (`isOpenEmbedding_infinitesimalNeighborhoodFromOne`).
- `formalCompletionSheafObj n` transports `𝒪_{Y_(n+1)}` to `|Y|` as
  `h.functor.op ⋙ 𝒪_{Y_(n+1)}` (sections over `V` are sections over the image of `V`), with the
  sheaf condition from `TopCat.Presheaf.isSheaf_of_isOpenEmbedding`. This is the structure sheaf
  of `Y_(n+1)`, i.e. `(𝒪_X / I^(n+1))|_Y`.
- `formalCompletionSheafMap n` is `Scheme.Hom.appLE` of `infinitesimalNeighborhoodSucc n :
  Y_(n+1) ⟶ Y_(n+2)`, which avoids `eqToHom` between images.

## Locality proof

Stalks of an inverse limit of sheaves are not inverse limits of stalks, so the proof goes through
sections:

1. `Hartshorne.Scheme.Hom.isUnit_of_isUnit_appLE`: for any morphism of schemes `f : A ⟶ B` and an
   open `W` mapping onto `U`, a section over `U` whose pullback to `W` is a unit is a unit
   (stalk maps of `f` are local; units are detected on stalks).
2. Hence each transition map reflects units, so a compatible family whose `n = 1` term is a unit
   has all terms units; `Hartshorne.isUnit_of_isLimit` (in a limit of commutative rings an
   element whose projections are all units is a unit) gives `isUnit_formalCompletionSheaf_iff`.
   No nilpotence or geometric series is needed.
3. For a germ whose image in the stalk of `𝒪_Y` is a unit, `RingedSpace.isUnit_res_of_isUnit_germ`
   gives a neighbourhood where the `𝒪_Y`-section is a unit; by step 2 the section of the limit is
   a unit there. So the stalk map to the local ring `𝒪_{Y,y}` is a local homomorphism, and
   `RingHom.domain_isLocalRing` makes the stalk local: its non-units are the preimage of the
   maximal ideal of `𝒪_{Y,y}`.

## Friction

- `TopCat.Sheaf C X` and `Sheaf (Opens.grothendieckTopology X) C` are not reducibly equal, so
  `PreservesLimit (formalCompletionDiagram I) (sheafToPresheaf _ _)` is not found by instance
  search even though `PreservesLimitsOfShape ℕᵒᵖ (sheafToPresheaf _ _)` is; the proof takes the
  witness from the shape instance.
- `Scheme.restrict` for the transported sheaf caused a kernel deterministic timeout; the direct
  presheaf `h.functor.op ⋙ 𝒪` does not.
- Leaving the opens of `Scheme.Hom.isUnit_of_isUnit_appLE` as `_` made elaboration time out;
  giving them explicitly is instant.
