# Strict transforms and the classical affine blowup

## Status: partial (definition and closure description proved; classical comparison open)

Module `Hartshorne.Scheme.BlowupStrictTransform` (sorry-free, standard axioms only).

### Proved

- Definition of the strict transform, for a morphism `f : Y ⟶ X` of Noetherian schemes and a
  coherent ideal `I` on `X`:
  `Hartshorne.QuasicoherentIdealSubsheaf.strictTransform` (the blowup of `f⁻¹I · 𝒪_Y`),
  `strictTransformProjection` (`π'`), `strictTransformι` (the map `f̃ : Ỹ ⟶ X̃` of Corollary 7.15,
  chosen from `existsUnique_blowup_map`), `strictTransformι_blowupProjection` (`π ∘ f̃ = f ∘ π'`),
  `eq_strictTransformι` (uniqueness), and the instance `isClosedImmersion_strictTransformι` (for a
  closed immersion `f`, from `isClosedImmersion_of_blowup_map`).
- Closure description, in general (no integrality, no `Y ⊄ V(I)` hypothesis needed; if `Y ⊆ V(I)`
  both sides are empty):
  `ker_strictTransformι`: the kernel of `f̃` equals the kernel (scheme-theoretic image ideal) of
  `pullback.snd ((f ⁻¹ᵁ I.centerCompl).ι ≫ f) π`, i.e. of `π⁻¹(Y \ V(I)) = (Y \ V(I)) ×_X X̃ ⟶ X̃`;
  `strictTransformIsoClosure` (+ `strictTransformIsoClosure_hom_imageι`): for closed immersions,
  `Ỹ` is isomorphic over `X̃` to that scheme-theoretic image;
  `range_strictTransformι`: on underlying sets, `f̃(Ỹ)` is the closure of `π⁻¹(Y \ V(I))`.
  The article's Example 7.15.1 case (`X = 𝔸ⁿ`, center the origin) is an instance; no separate
  `𝔸ⁿ` declaration was added (it would carry the book's integrality / `Y ≠ {P}` hypotheses
  unused).
- Helpers: `Hartshorne.isSchemeTheoreticallyDominant_ι_of_isLocallyPrincipalNonZeroDivisor` (the
  complement of the support of an ideal locally generated by one non-zero-divisor is
  scheme-theoretically dense; Mathlib's `IsSchemeTheoreticallyDominant`), `centerCompl_inverseImage`,
  `isSchemeTheoreticallyDominant_blowupProjection_preimage_centerCompl`.

### Route taken (differs from the article's proof sketch)

The proof does not use the density of `Y - {P}` in the integral variety `Y`. Instead: by
Proposition 7.13(b) (`isIso_blowupProjection_restrict_centerCompl`) for both `π` and `π'`, the map
`π'⁻¹(Y \ V(I)) ⟶ (Y \ V(I)) ×_X X̃` induced by `π'` and `f̃` is an isomorphism; by Proposition
7.13(a) (`isLocallyPrincipalNonZeroDivisor_exceptionalIdeal`) the open `π'⁻¹(Y \ V(I))` is
scheme-theoretically dense in `Ỹ`, so `ker f̃ = ker (π'⁻¹(Y \ V(I)) ⟶ X̃)` by Mathlib's
`Scheme.Hom.ker_comp` / `IdealSheafData.map_bot`.

### Remaining goal: the Chapter-I comparison

Identify, for `k` algebraically closed, `Y ⊆ 𝔸ⁿ_k` an affine variety through the origin with
`Y ≠ {0}`, the scheme `Ỹ` (strict transform for the ideal of the origin) with the scheme associated
to the classical strict transform recorded with the Chapter-I blowup (`Hartshorne.blowing_up`,
`strictTransform` in `Hartshorne.Rational.BlowingUpStrictTransform`), compatibly with the maps to
`𝔸ⁿ_k`. Needed pieces, none present on main:

1. the associated scheme `varietyAssociatedScheme` of the classical affine space `𝔸ⁿ` is Mathlib's
   `𝔸(Fin n; Spec k)` (affine comparison exists for affine varieties via `Spec`, but not this
   identification with the Mathlib affine space);
2. the associated scheme of the classical incidence blowup `blowupSet` is the Proj of the Rees
   algebra of `(x₁, …, xₙ)`, i.e. `Proj k[x][y]/(xᵢyⱼ - xⱼyᵢ)`, over `𝔸ⁿ_k`;
3. for a locally closed classical subvariety `Z` of a variety `V`, the associated scheme of `Z` is
   the reduced closed (or locally closed) subscheme of the associated scheme of `V` with the
   corresponding support; with `range_strictTransformι` and reducedness/integrality of `Ỹ` this
   identifies `Ỹ` with the associated scheme of the classical closure.

Item 3 is exactly the comparison done in the held article "Classical varieties are the
quasi-projective integral schemes" (associated-variety-essential-image), so it was not attempted
here. Route once available: prove `Ỹ` integral for integral `Y ⊄ V(I)` (dense open
`π'⁻¹(Y \ V(I)) ≅ Y \ V(I)` integral, plus scheme-theoretic density above), then apply 1–3.
