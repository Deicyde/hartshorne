# An ample power descends from a curve normalization

## Status: blocked on missing prerequisites (no Lean yet)

No Lean was written. The statement cannot be recorded faithfully yet, and the
proof needs several results the roadmap has only as prose items.

### Needed to state the result

- Very ampleness. The hypothesis "$L$ very ample on $\widetilde X$" needs the
  definition in the article on relative twisting sheaves and very ampleness
  (af_407186ce584db2341c2b88f8). That article is not formalized, and neither is
  its dependency on pullback of twisting sheaves (af_0a85053c2012b17661490eb8).
  The missing Depends on link to it is resolved by the roadmap revision. The normalization
  (`Hartshorne.absoluteNormalization`, `Hartshorne.absoluteNormalizationMap`)
  and Picard pullback (`Hartshorne.Ringed.Pic.pullback`) needed for the
  conclusion already exist.

### Needed for the proof (roadmap revision: what is linked and what stays prose)

1. For an integral Noetherian scheme (enough: an integral curve of finite type
   over a field), the normalization map is an isomorphism over the normal locus.
   On a curve the normal locus is the nonsingular locus, its complement is
   finite, and the complement is closed. Only the case $X$ normal is in Lean
   (`isIso_fromNormalization_of_isNormal`).
   Resolved by the roadmap revision: the bridged article
   `schemes/normalization-and-exercises/normalization-iso-over-normal-open`
   (af_4ff6678fe30f65e86a6bb8b4) states the isomorphism over a normal open and is
   linked from Proof depends on, and so is Corollary II.8.16
   (`dense-nonsingular-locus`, af_e2d480a9e888caa28bd5ec9b; $X$ is a variety in
   Hartshorne's sense, being integral, separated and of finite type over $k$).
   "Regular local rings are normal; a proper closed subset of an integral curve is
   finite" stays a prose item.
2. Divisors of sections and avoiding finitely many points. For $L$ very ample
   over an infinite field and a finite set $S$ of closed points, there is a
   global section (the pullback of a linear form) that vanishes at no point of
   $S$. Its zero locus is an effective Cartier divisor $D$ with
   $\mathcal O(D) \cong L$. Neither the effective-divisor-of-a-section
   construction nor the isomorphism $\mathcal O(D) \cong L$ has an article. With
   $k$ algebraically closed this already works for $m = 1$.
   Partly resolved by the roadmap revision: Proposition II.7.7
   (`divisor-of-invertible-section`, af_1bc813d687359f0e05cea7a7, sections of
   $\mathcal O(D_0)$ versus effective divisors linearly equivalent to $D_0$ on a
   nonsingular projective variety) is linked from Proof depends on; it applies to
   $\widetilde X$, which is normal of dimension one, hence nonsingular (Theorem
   I.6.2A, `dvr-characterizations`), and projective because $L$ is very ample and
   $\widetilde X$ is proper (finite over the proper $X$). Choosing a section that avoids finitely many points
   stays a prose item: Hartshorne gives it no numbered statement and it is a
   one-line linear-algebra argument over an infinite field.
3. Descending and pulling back Cartier divisors. If $\nu$ is an isomorphism
   over an open set $U$ containing the image of $\operatorname{Supp} D$, then
   $D$ is $\nu^* D_0$ for a Cartier divisor $D_0$ on $X$ (with local equation
   $1$ away from $\nu(\operatorname{Supp} D)$), and
   $\nu^* \mathcal O_X(D_0) \cong \mathcal O_{\widetilde X}(\nu^* D_0)$. Pullback
   of Cartier divisors along a dominant morphism of integral schemes, and its
   compatibility with the associated sheaves, has no article. The existing
   pullback-compatibility article (af_0faaf08ed4fc25eccdda1218) covers only Weil
   divisors on nonsingular curves and is unformalized. Left as a prose item by the
   roadmap revision: Hartshorne states no general pullback of Cartier divisors,
   and only this article would use it.

### Next route

Formalize the very-ample definition (and the twisting-sheaf pullback under it)
first, and `normalization-iso-over-normal-open` (its note names a route through
the existing `U = X` case). The remaining prose items (regular implies normal and finiteness of the
singular locus of a curve, a section avoiding finitely many points, Cartier descent along
$\nu$) are proved inside this leaf or split out by Roadmap if they grow. After
that, the proof is a short assembly: take $m = 1$, use a hyperplane section
that avoids the preimage of the singular locus, then descend.
