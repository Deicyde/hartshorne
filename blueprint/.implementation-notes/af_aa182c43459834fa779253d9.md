# Classical varieties are the quasi-projective integral schemes

## Reading of the statement

The existing variety-to-scheme functor is defined on all abstract varieties
with an affine-open basis. That category is larger than Hartshorne's category
of classical varieties: it contains, for example, the affine line with a doubled
origin, whose associated scheme is not separated, hence not quasi-projective.
The faithful reading restricts the functor to the full subcategory of varieties
isomorphic to a quasi-projective variety in some $\mathbb{P}^n$ (respectively to
a projective variety) and describes the essential image of the restricted
functor.

## Partial progress

The module `Hartshorne.Scheme.AssociatedVarietyEssentialImage` (sorry-free,
axioms only `propext`, `Classical.choice`, `Quot.sound`; not recorded in the
article frontmatter) proves the first inclusion of both clauses:

- `Hartshorne.Variety.IsQuasiProjective`, `Hartshorne.Variety.IsProjective` and
  the object properties `Hartshorne.SchemeVarietyCat.isQuasiProjective`,
  `Hartshorne.SchemeVarietyCat.isProjective`: being isomorphic to a
  quasi-projective (respectively projective) variety.
- `Hartshorne.isIntegral_varietyAssociatedScheme`: $t(V)$ is integral for every
  variety $V$ (the generic point lies in every nonempty open; sections are rings
  of $k$-valued functions, hence reduced).
- `Hartshorne.projective_structureMorphism_ofProjective`: for a projective
  variety $Y$, $t(Y) \to \operatorname{Spec} k$ is projective, via
  $t(Y) \cong \operatorname{Proj} S(Y)$, $S(Y)_0 = k$, and generation of $S(Y)$
  in degree one.
- `Hartshorne.quasiProjective_structureMorphism_ofQuasiProjective`: for a
  quasi-projective variety $Y$, the inclusion into its closure induces an open
  immersion $t(Y) \to t(\overline{Y})$ over $k$, so $t(Y) \to \operatorname{Spec} k$
  is quasi-projective.
- `Hartshorne.isIntegral_and_quasiProjective_of_mem_essImage` and
  `Hartshorne.isIntegral_and_projective_of_mem_essImage`: every object of the
  essential image of the restricted functor is integral with quasi-projective
  (respectively projective) structure morphism.

The projective step uses the theorem that a $\operatorname{Proj}$ generated in
degree one is projective (article
`schemes/proper-and-projective/generated-proj-projective`), which the article's
dependency lists do not yet reach.

## Remaining goal

The converse inclusions: an integral scheme $X$ over $k$ with projective
(respectively quasi-projective) structure morphism is isomorphic over $k$ to
$t(Y)$ for a projective (respectively quasi-projective) variety $Y$.

## Blocker

1. The project's relative projective space $\mathbb{P}^n_{\operatorname{Spec} k}$
   is the base change of $\mathbb{P}^n_{\mathbb{Z}}$. Neither the project nor
   Mathlib at the current pin identifies it, over $\operatorname{Spec} k$, with
   $\operatorname{Proj} k[x_0,\dots,x_n]$, or with $t(\mathbb{P}^n(k))$; Mathlib has
   no base change for $\operatorname{Proj}$. Without it a closed immersion
   $X \to \mathbb{P}^n_{\operatorname{Spec} k}$ cannot be compared with the
   classical $\mathbb{P}^n(k)$.
2. For a closed subvariety $Y \subseteq \mathbb{P}^n(k)$, nothing yet shows that
   $t(Y) \to t(\mathbb{P}^n(k))$ (or
   $\operatorname{Proj} S(Y) \to \operatorname{Proj} S(\mathbb{P}^n)$, transported
   through the Exercise 2.14(d) isomorphisms) is a closed immersion whose image
   is the closure of the classical points of $Y$.

## Next route

- Projective case: from item 1, view $X$ as a closed integral subscheme of
  $t(\mathbb{P}^n(k))$; its classical points form a closed set $Y$, irreducible
  because closed points are dense in $X$ (finite type over $k$). By item 2,
  $t(Y)$ is a reduced closed subscheme with the same support as $X$. Two reduced
  closed subschemes with the same support are isomorphic over the ambient
  scheme: lift one closed immersion through the other using kernels (as in the
  smallest-reduced-induced theorem), then apply Mathlib's
  `isIso_of_isClosedImmersion_of_surjective`. Compatibility with the structure
  morphisms follows from uniqueness of the structure morphism of $t(Y)$.
- Quasi-projective case: factor $X \to Z \to \mathbb{P}^n$ (open, then closed);
  replace $Z$ by the reduced induced subscheme on the closure of $X$, which is
  integral, apply the projective case to get $Z \cong t(Y')$, and match $X$ with
  $t(U)$ for the corresponding open $U \subseteq Y'$ using the open immersion
  $t(U) \to t(Y')$ built in this module and Mathlib's
  `IsOpenImmersion.isoOfRangeEq`.
- Missing roadmap prerequisites to add: "$\mathbb{P}^n_{\operatorname{Spec} A}
  \cong \operatorname{Proj} A[x_0,\dots,x_n]$ over $\operatorname{Spec} A$" and
  "a closed subvariety of a projective variety gives a closed subscheme of the
  associated scheme".
