# Projective-line point count

Finished state (candidate, pending review). The result is
`Hartshorne.pointCountSequence_projectiveLine` in
`Hartshorne.Weil.ProjectiveLinePointCount`: for a finite field `k` and `r : ℕ+`,
`pointCountSequence k (projectiveSpaceOverScheme 1 (Spec k))
(projectiveSpaceOverSchemeProjection 1 (Spec k)) _ r = Nat.card k ^ r + 1`.

The scheme in `pointCountSequence` is literally the scheme in
`Hartshorne.ProjectiveLinePoints.Point k K`, namely
`projectiveSpaceOverScheme 1 (Spec k)` with `projectiveSpaceOverSchemeProjection`,
so no extra identification of models of `ℙ¹` is needed. The over-category
morphisms counted by `pointCountSequence` are repackaged as the commuting
morphisms of `Point` by a private equivalence (`Over.homMk` / `Over.w`).

Ingredients:

- `Hartshorne.projectiveSpaceOverSchemeProjection_finiteType` (new, in the same
  module) supplies the finite-type proof, from
  `projectiveSpaceOverSchemeProjection_isProper`.
- `Hartshorne.ProjectiveLinePoints.equivProjectiveSpace` identifies the points
  with `ProjectiveSpace K (Fin 2)`.
- Mathlib's `Projectivization.card_of_finrank_two` counts `#K + 1` lines.
- `Hartshorne.finiteFieldExtension_natCard` gives `#K = q^r`.

Route note: the article's proof sketch splits the classical projective line
into the affine chart (`q^r` points) and the point at infinity. The Lean uses
Mathlib's count of lines in a two-dimensional space instead
(`(q^{2r} - 1)/(q^r - 1) = q^r + 1`), which is the same classical count.
