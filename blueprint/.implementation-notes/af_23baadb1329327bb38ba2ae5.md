# Linear equivalence is isomorphism of divisor sheaves

## Lean

Module `Hartshorne.Scheme.CartierLinearEquivalenceSheaf`. Linear equivalence is the existing
`Scheme.CartierDivisor.LinearEquivalent` (difference in the range of the principal-divisor map),
from the Cartier class group article; `O_X(D)` is `Scheme.CartierDivisor.sheaf`, and the
isomorphism is in `X.Modules`, so the embeddings into `K_X` play no role.

- `isoOfEqAddPrincipal`: if `D = D' + (f)` for a global section `f` of `K_X^*`, multiplication by
  `f` is an isomorphism `O_X(D) ≅ O_X(D')` with inverse multiplication by `f⁻¹`.
- `exists_mul_of_hom`: every morphism `O_X(D₁) ⟶ O_X(D₂)` is multiplication by a global section
  `m` of `K_X`. On an open carrying a local equation `f` of `D₁`, the multiplier is `φ(f⁻¹) f`
  (`localMultiplier`, `hom_app_val_eq_mul_localMultiplier`); the local multipliers glue.
- `linearEquivalent_of_iso`: the multipliers of `φ` and `φ⁻¹` multiply to `1`, so `m` is a
  global unit of `K_X`, and comparing local equations with `restrict_eq_of_ratio` gives
  `D₁ - D₂ = (m)`.

## Route

The converse does not go through Proposition 6.13(b) (`subIsoTensorDual`) and a trivialization of
`O_X(D₁ - D₂)`. It proves directly that an abstract isomorphism `O_X(D₁) ≅ O_X(D₂)` is
multiplication by a global unit of `K_X`. This is the same local-generator argument as the
source's trivialization step, and it avoids the tensor and dual identifications.
