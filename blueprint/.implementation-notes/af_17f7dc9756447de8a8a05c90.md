# The projective-space case of Bertini

## Partial progress

The module `Hartshorne.Scheme.ProjectiveSpaceRegular` (sorry-free, not recorded
in the article frontmatter) proves the regularity half of the statement:

- `Hartshorne.isRegularAt_of_isAffineOpen`: a point lying in an affine open $U$
  of a scheme $X$ with $\Gamma(X,U)$ a regular ring is a regular point, since
  $\mathcal{O}_{X,x}$ is the localization of $\Gamma(X,U)$ at a prime.
- `Hartshorne.projectiveSpaceScheme_isRegular`: $\mathbb{P}^m_A$ is a regular
  scheme for every regular ring $A$ and every $m$, in particular for $A = k$ a
  field. The coordinate charts $D_+(x_j)$ have rings of sections
  $A[x_0,\ldots,x_m]_{(x_j)} \cong A[m \text{ variables}]$ (the project's
  unrecorded chart isomorphism in `Hartshorne.Scheme.ProjectiveSpaceChart`), and
  Mathlib proves that polynomial rings in finitely many variables over a
  regular ring are regular.

## Blocker

The statement speaks of hyperplane sections of $\mathbb{P}^n_k$, that is, the
closed subscheme $V_+(h) = \operatorname{Proj}(k[x_0,\ldots,x_n]/(h))$ for a
nonzero linear form $h$. Neither dependency of this article (projective space
over a ring; regular points and nonsingular schemes), nor their transitive
dependencies, provides the closed subscheme of $\operatorname{Proj} S$ cut out
by a homogeneous ideal. The project has it in
`blueprint/roadmap/schemes/normalization-and-exercises/proj-closed-subscheme-truncation.md`
(graded quotient $S/I$ and the closed immersion
$\operatorname{Proj}(S/I) \to \operatorname{Proj} S$), which this article does
not reach.

## Next route

Statement: for a field $k$, $n \ge 1$ and a nonzero homogeneous $h$ of degree
one, $\operatorname{Proj}(S/(h)) \cong \mathbb{P}^{n-1}_k$ and
$\operatorname{Proj}(S/(h))$ is regular (regularity then follows from the
isomorphism and the partial result above). For the isomorphism:

1. Choose a $k$-linear automorphism of $k^{n+1}$ carrying the coordinate
   functional $x_0$ to $h$; the induced substitution is a graded
   $k$-algebra automorphism of $S = k[x_0,\ldots,x_n]$ carrying $(x_0)$ onto
   $(h)$.
2. The graded quotient $S/(x_0)$ is graded-isomorphic to $k[x_1,\ldots,x_n]$.
3. A graded ring isomorphism induces an isomorphism of $\operatorname{Proj}$
   schemes; Mathlib at the current pin has no functoriality of
   $\operatorname{Proj}$, but the project's partial-map construction for
   graded homomorphisms (used for the closed immersion
   $\operatorname{Proj}(S/I) \to \operatorname{Proj} S$) applies to a graded
   isomorphism and its inverse.
