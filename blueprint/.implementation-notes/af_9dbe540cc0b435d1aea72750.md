# The dimension-codimension formula

## Status

## Statement (checked to elaborate)

With `X : Scheme`, `f : X ⟶ Spec (.of k)`, `[IsIntegral X]`,
`hf : Hartshorne.FiniteType f`, `Y : Set X` closed and nonempty:
`topologicalKrullDim Y + (Hartshorne.codim Y : WithBot ℕ∞) = topologicalKrullDim X`,
with `Y` carrying the subspace topology.

## Proof route

- Reduce to irreducible closed `Z`: every chain in `Y` has an irreducible
  closed top element, so `dim Y` is the supremum of `dim Z` over irreducible
  closed `Z ⊆ Y`, and `codim Y` is by definition the infimum of their
  coheights. Hence it suffices that `dim Z + coheight Z = dim X` for every
  irreducible closed `Z` (all quantities finite).
- Every nonempty affine open `W = Spec B` has `dim B = dim X`: a maximal ideal
  of `B` has height `dim B`; its point is closed in `X` because `X` is a
  Jacobson space (finite type over a field); then apply the dimension at a
  closed point and identify the stalk dimension with the height.
- For irreducible closed `Z` and an affine open `W = Spec B` meeting it, with
  `q` the prime of `Z ∩ W`: the coheight of `Z` in `X` equals the height of
  `q` (every irreducible closed set containing `Z` meets `W`; use
  `IrreducibleCloseds.orderIsoOfIsOpenEmbedding`), and `dim (Z ∩ W) =
  dim B/q`. The dimension formula gives `dim B/q = dim X - ht q`.
- `dim Z ≥ dim (Z ∩ W)` by restriction to the open subset. For the reverse,
  given a chain in `Z`, choose an affine open around a point of its bottom
  element; the whole chain meets it, so its length is at most
  `dim B'/q' = dim X - coheight Z`, as in the proof of the dimension at a
  closed point.
