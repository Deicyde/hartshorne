# Projective cycles are rationally equivalent to degree times a linear space

## Status

Proved, sorry-free, in module `Hartshorne.Intersection.ProjectiveCycleLinearSpaceEquivalence`
(imported by the library root), standard axioms only. Candidate `lean:` (pending review):

- `Hartshorne.rationallyEquivalent_projectiveDegree_smul_linearSubspaceCycle`: for `k`
  algebraically closed, `m ≤ n`, `y : Proj k[x₀,…,xₙ]` with
  `codim (closure {y}) = n - m` and `d : ℤ` with `(d : ℚ)` equal to the projective degree
  (`Hartshorne.projectiveDegree`, from the Hilbert polynomial) of the zero set of the homogeneous
  prime of `y`, the cycle `pointCodimensionCycle y hy` is `RationallyEquivalent` to
  `d • linearSubspaceCycle n m rfl hm`.
- `Hartshorne.chowGroup_mk_pointCodimensionCycle_eq_projectiveDegree_smul`: the same as an
  equation `[Y] = d • [L_m]` in `chowGroup … (n - m)` (classes via `QuotientAddGroup.mk`).

`L_m = V(x_{m+1}, …, xₙ)` stands for the article's `ℙᵐ`.

## Route

Not the article's projection-family sketch. The Lean applies
`Hartshorne.rationallyEquivalent_degree_smul_linearSubspace_of_exists` (unrecorded helper in
`ProjectiveLinearSubspaceCycle`, which uses `deg L_m = 1`,
`Hartshorne.projectiveSpectrumPointDegree_linearSubspacePoint`) to

1. `Hartshorne.exists_rationallyEquivalent_zsmul_linearSubspace`
   (projective-space-linear-subspace-generation): `[Y] ~ a·[L_m]` for some integer `a`;
2. `Hartshorne.cycleProjectiveDegree_eq_zero_of_mem_rationalEquivalenceSubgroup`
   (projective-cycle-degree-rational-invariance): degree vanishes on rational equivalence;

and converts the integer degree `Hartshorne.projectiveSpectrumPointDegree y` to the Hilbert
polynomial degree with `Hartshorne.projectiveSpectrumPointDegree_cast`
(projective-weil-divisor-degree).

The projection-family route would need the family form of rational equivalence (Exercise A.6.1,
OUT in coverage, no article), proper pushforward of rational equivalence from `ℙ¹ × ℙⁿ`
(proper-pushforward-preserves-rational-equivalence), and the generic degree of a finite linear
projection; none of these is used.
