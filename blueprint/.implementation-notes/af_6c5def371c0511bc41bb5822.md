# The class group of projective space: missing prerequisites

## Status

Not started in Lean. The leaf is blocked on prerequisites that the roadmap
does not supply; there is no partial Lean worth keeping yet.

## Setting

The statement concerns the scheme $\mathbb{P}^n_k = \operatorname{Proj} k[x_0,\ldots,x_n]$
from the projective-space-over-a-ring article, over an arbitrary field $k$.
The class group of the weil-linear-equivalence article is only defined for a
scheme together with a proof of condition $(*)$.

The listed dependency on "projective codimension one is a hypersurface"
(Exercise I.2.8) is a statement about classical projective varieties: subsets
of the Chapter I projective space over an algebraically closed field, with the
classical dimension. It says nothing about points of the scheme
$\operatorname{Proj}$, and its field hypothesis is stronger than the
article's. No article reachable from this leaf relates the two settings.

## Missing prerequisites

1. **Condition $(*)$ for $\mathbb{P}^n_k$.** Without it, $\operatorname{Cl}(\mathbb{P}^n_k)$
   cannot even be stated. It needs:
   - Noetherian: the finite cover by the charts $D_+(x_i)$, each the spectrum
     of a polynomial ring in $n$ variables;
   - integral: $\operatorname{Proj}$ of a graded domain;
   - separated: pinned Mathlib already provides this for every
     $\operatorname{Proj}$;
   - regular in codimension one: for example via normality of the charts
     (polynomial rings are UFDs) together with the normal-implies-$(*)$
     lemma proved in the affine UFD class-group article (Proposition 6.2).
     That article is not among this leaf's dependencies.

   Pinned Mathlib proves no integrality, Noetherian, or dimension results for
   $\operatorname{Proj}$.
2. **Prime divisors of $\mathbb{P}^n_k$ are hypersurfaces.** This is a scheme
   version of Exercise I.2.8 over an arbitrary field. Points of coheight one
   in $\operatorname{Proj} S$, for $S = k[x_0,\ldots,x_n]$, correspond to
   relevant homogeneous primes of height one. These are exactly the principal
   ideals $(f)$ with $f$ irreducible, homogeneous, and of positive degree,
   unique up to a nonzero scalar. The degree of a Weil divisor is defined
   through this correspondence, so the statement cannot be written faithfully
   without it. The purely algebraic half (an irreducible generator of a
   homogeneous principal ideal can be taken homogeneous of positive degree)
   already exists as a private helper in the Exercise I.2.8 module and could
   be made public in a new article.
3. **Function field and valuations.** The function field of $\mathbb{P}^n_k$
   consists of the quotients $g/h$ of homogeneous polynomials of equal
   degree. The valuation of $g/h$ at the prime divisor $Z(f)$ is the
   $f$-adic order of $g$ minus that of $h$. Pinned Mathlib identifies the
   stalk of $\operatorname{Proj}$ with the degree-zero homogeneous
   localization, but it computes neither the function field nor this
   discrete valuation.
4. **The hyperplane divisor** $H$: the prime divisor of the homogeneous
   prime $(x_0)$. This becomes routine once item 2 is in place.

## Suggested roadmap repair

Add articles for items 1–3 (projective space satisfies $(*)$; prime divisors
of $\mathbb{P}^n_k$ are hypersurfaces of a well-defined degree; the valuation of
a homogeneous fraction at a hypersurface). Make this leaf depend on them. The
dependency on the classical Exercise I.2.8 can then be dropped or kept only
as motivation. Once these exist, the proof follows Hartshorne. For
$D=\sum n_i Z(g_i)$, the divisor $D-\deg(D)H$ is the divisor of
$\prod g_i^{n_i}/x_0^{\deg D}$. Principal divisors have degree $0$ by
item 3. Hence degree is well defined on classes, and it is surjective
because it sends $H$ to $1$. It is injective because a degree-zero divisor
is the divisor of $\prod g_i^{n_i}$, which is a quotient of equal-degree
homogeneous polynomials.
