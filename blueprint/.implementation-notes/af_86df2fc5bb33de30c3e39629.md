# A regular local ring is an integral domain

## Status

New article from the regular-local-differential-criterion roadmap revision.
The project already proves the result, sorry-free, but the article records no
declaration yet: a later Formalize pass records it after independent review.

## Candidate Lean

In the module `Hartshorne.Scheme.RegularLocalCohenMacaulay` (owned by the
regular-local-implies-cohen-macaulay article, which records only
`IsRegularLocalRing.isCohenMacaulayLocalRing`):

- `Hartshorne.RegularLocal.isDomain`: `IsDomain R` for
  `[CommRing R] [IsRegularLocalRing R]`. This is the article statement.
- `Hartshorne.RegularLocal.isDomain_of_spanFinrank_eq`: the induction on the
  number `n` of generators of the maximal ideal.

The proof also uses the helpers
`Hartshorne.RegularLocal.isRegularLocalRing_quotient_of_notMem_sq`
($A/(x)$ is regular of one dimension less for $x\in\mathfrak m\setminus\mathfrak m^2$),
`Hartshorne.RegularLocal.exists_mem_maximalIdeal_notMem_sq_notMem_minimalPrimes`
(prime avoidance) and `Hartshorne.RegularLocal.isDomain_of_isPrime_bot`. For the
cotangent-dimension bound it uses Mathlib's
`ringKrullDim_le_spanFinrank_maximalIdeal` rather than the project's
`Hartshorne.ringKrullDim_le_finrank_cotangentSpace`, and for Krull's theorem
Mathlib's `ringKrullDim_le_ringKrullDim_quotient_add_card`.

The pinned Mathlib has the definition of `IsRegularLocalRing` but no domain
theorem for it (only the instance that a principal-ideal local domain is
regular, in `Mathlib/RingTheory/RegularLocalRing/Defs.lean`).

## Source

The locator is the proof of Theorem II.8.8, printed pp. 174–175, where
Hartshorne takes the quotient field of the regular local ring $B$. The vault's
source notes (`blueprint/sources/hartshorne-ii-8.md`) summarize that page but
do not transcribe Hartshorne's citation for this fact, so the exact Matsumura
reference he gives was not checked against the book. Matsumura, *Commutative
Ring Theory*, Theorem 14.3, states the result.

## Later consumers

The regular-locally-factorial article also uses
`Hartshorne.RegularLocal.isDomain` and links this article under Proof depends
on. The regular-localization proof does not use it directly: it
appears inside `Hartshorne.RegularLocal.exists_isWeaklyRegular_of_spanFinrank_eq`,
the helper behind residue-field-projective-dimension, whose note records the
route difference. The bertini-section-irreducible article lists "Regular local
rings are integral domains" as an unlinked proof prerequisite.
