# Regular schemes are locally factorial

## Prior implementation guidance

The algebraic implication “regular local ring implies UFD” (Auslander–Buchsbaum)
is not available in the pinned Mathlib checkout. It remains a sourced
prerequisite rather than an asserted upstream fact. The later Weil–Cartier
comparison is deliberately not part of this node. Once that algebraic theorem
exists, this node is immediate: the stalks are regular local rings, regular
local rings are domains (already in the project), and the scheme-level
predicate asks exactly for domain and UFD stalks.

## Partial progress (bounded attempt)

Module `Hartshorne.Scheme.RegularLocalFactorial` (sorry-free, imported by the
library root), namespace `Hartshorne.RegularLocalFactorial`:

- `uniqueFactorizationMonoid_of_ringKrullDim_le_one`: a regular local ring of
  dimension at most one is a UFD (base case of the induction).
- `prime_of_mem_maximalIdeal_of_notMem_sq`: in a regular local ring, an element
  of the maximal ideal outside its square is a prime element.
- `uniqueFactorizationMonoid_iff_localizationAway`: for such an element `x`,
  the ring is a UFD iff its localization away from `x` is (Nagata's criterion,
  via Mathlib's `UniqueFactorizationMonoid.iff_localizationAway_of_prime`).
- `exists_mem_maximalIdeal_notMem_sq`: positive dimension gives such an `x`.
- `exists_mem_forall_exists_mul_eq_of_isPrincipal_map`,
  `isUnit_map_of_forall_exists_mul_eq`: a nonzero finitely generated ideal of a
  domain that becomes principal at every maximal ideal is an invertible
  fractional ideal (this local-to-global step is missing from Mathlib).
- `isPrincipal_of_linearEquiv`: an ideal isomorphic to the ring is principal.
- `isPrincipal_of_isStablyFree_of_forall_isPrincipal_map`: a nonzero stably
  free ideal of a Noetherian domain that is principal at every maximal ideal is
  principal (invertible and stably free gives free, Mathlib's
  `Module.free_of_isStablyFree_of_invertible`).
- `uniqueFactorizationMonoid_of_isStablyFree`: a Noetherian domain whose
  localizations at maximal ideals are UFDs and whose height-one primes are
  stably free is a UFD.

Mathlib already provides Kaplansky's criterion, the height-one-principal UFD
criterion, Nagata's criterion, "stably free and invertible implies free", and
the projective dimension of a quotient by a regular sequence. The project
already provides that a regular local ring is a domain and that the quotient by
an element of `𝔪 \ 𝔪²` is regular of one smaller dimension (module of the
Cohen–Macaulay node).

## Remaining goal

Every regular local ring is a UFD. With the lemmas above, the induction on
`d = dim R` closes once, for `x ∈ 𝔪 \ 𝔪²` and `S = R[1/x]`:

1. every localization of `S` at a maximal ideal is a UFD: it is a localization
   of `R` at a prime `𝔮 ≠ 𝔪`, which is regular (regular-localization node) of
   dimension `< d`, so the induction hypothesis applies;
2. every height-one prime of `S` is stably free: it is the localization of a
   finitely generated `R`-module, which has a finite free resolution because a
   regular local ring has finite global dimension (finitely generated
   projectives over a local ring are free); localization keeps a finite free
   resolution, and a projective module with a finite free resolution is stably
   free.

## Next route / proposed split

The missing pieces are article-sized and need Roadmap nodes:

- Serre, one direction: every finitely generated module over a regular local
  ring has projective dimension at most `dim R` (Matsumura, *Commutative Ring
  Theory*, Theorem 19.2). Route: the maximal ideal is generated by a regular
  sequence (the Cohen–Macaulay node's module builds one), so the residue field
  has projective dimension `d` by Mathlib's quotient-by-regular-sequence
  result; then bound the projective dimension of a finitely generated module by
  that of the residue field (minimal resolutions, Tor against the residue
  field).
- A finitely generated projective module with a finite free resolution is
  stably free, and finite free resolutions localize (Matsumura, CRT, §19–20).
- Localizations of regular local rings are regular (existing node
  regular-localization).
- The algebraic theorem: a regular local ring is a UFD (Matsumura [2], Theorem
  48, p. 142; Matsumura, CRT, Theorem 20.3), depending on the three pieces
  above and on the Cohen–Macaulay node; it would record the lemmas of this
  module as supporting declarations. This node then depends on it.
