# Regular schemes are locally factorial

## Prior implementation guidance

The algebraic implication “regular local ring implies UFD” (Auslander–Buchsbaum,
the article's cited Matsumura Theorem 48) is not in the pinned Mathlib. The
later Weil–Cartier comparison is deliberately not part of this node. Once the
algebraic theorem exists, this node is immediate: the stalks are regular local
rings, regular local rings are domains (`Hartshorne.RegularLocal.isDomain`),
and the scheme-level predicate asks exactly for domain and UFD stalks.

## Partial progress (bounded attempt)

Module `Hartshorne.Scheme.RegularLocalFactorial` (sorry-free, imported by the
library root), namespace `Hartshorne.RegularLocalFactorial`. The algebraic
theorem is proved except for one input, the regularity of prime localizations
of regular local rings (the regular-localization node):

- `uniqueFactorizationMonoid_of_forall_isRegularLocalRing_localization`: every
  regular local ring is a UFD, given (as hypothesis `H1`) that every prime
  localization of a regular local ring is regular.

## Remaining goal

Only the regularity of prime localizations of regular local rings, which is the
existing regular-localization node (Hartshorne Theorem 8.14A background). Once
that node's declaration (stated as `IsRegularLocalRing (Localization.AtPrime p)`
for every prime `p` of a regular local ring `A : Type u`) is on main:

1. instantiate `H1` of
   `uniqueFactorizationMonoid_of_forall_isRegularLocalRing_localization` with
   it, giving “every regular local ring is a UFD”;
2. state this node as: a scheme all of whose stalks are regular local rings is
   locally factorial, proved pointwise by `Hartshorne.RegularLocal.isDomain`
   and step 1;
3. add the link-only Depends on edge to regular-localization (and to the
   Cohen–Macaulay node, whose module supplies the quotient-by-`x` lemmas).

No new article is needed: the remaining algebra is the article's cited
Matsumura Theorem 48, now supporting lemmas. If the regular-localization work
also lands a proof of the Serre bound, the three Serre declarations here
duplicate it and can be replaced by it through
`uniqueFactorizationMonoid_of_forall_hasProjectiveDimensionLE`.
