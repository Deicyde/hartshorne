# Localizations of regular local rings

## Status

`Hartshorne.RegularLocal.isRegularLocalRing_localization_atPrime` in
`Hartshorne.Scheme.SerreRegularity` (imported by the library root), with the
corollary `Hartshorne.RegularLocal.isRegularRing_of_isRegularLocalRing` (a
regular local ring is a regular ring; this is the TODO of Mathlib's

(`Hartshorne.RegularLocal.isDomain` appears only inside the helper behind

regular-local-ring-domain is added here.

## Proof route (as formalized)

1. Over a Noetherian local ring $R$ with residue field $k$, if
   $\operatorname{pd}_R k \le n$ then every finitely generated module has
   projective dimension at most $n$. Only Ext is used: vanishing of
   $\operatorname{Ext}^{>n}(-, L)$ passes through prime filtrations, and for a
   prime $\mathfrak q \ne \mathfrak m$ and $x \in \mathfrak m \setminus
   \mathfrak q$ the sequence $0 \to R/\mathfrak q \xrightarrow{x} R/\mathfrak q
   \to R/(\mathfrak q + x) \to 0$ with Nakayama on the finitely generated
   $\operatorname{Ext}^i(R/\mathfrak q, L)$ reduces to larger primes. Mathlib's
   `ModuleCat.hasProjectiveDimensionLT_of_forall_finite` converts the vanishing
   against finitely generated $L$ into the bound. No balanced Tor and no
   minimal resolutions are needed.
2. In a regular local ring the maximal ideal is generated by a regular
   sequence of length $\dim A$, so $\operatorname{pd} k = \dim A$.
3. Hence $\operatorname{pd}_A A/\mathfrak p \le \dim A$ and, localizing,
   $\operatorname{pd}_{A_\mathfrak p} \kappa(\mathfrak p) \le \dim A$.
4. Serre's criterion, by induction on a bound for $\operatorname{pd} k$: if $k$
   is projective then $\mathfrak m = 0$; if $\mathfrak m$ is associated to $B$,
   a minimal presentation shows every finitely generated module of finite
   projective dimension is free; otherwise prime avoidance gives a
   non-zero-divisor $x \in \mathfrak m \setminus \mathfrak m^2$, change of
   rings gives $\operatorname{pd}_{B/x}(\mathfrak m/x\mathfrak m) < n$, the
   residue field of $B/x$ is a direct summand of $\mathfrak m/x\mathfrak m$,
   so $B/x$ is regular by induction and then so is $B$.
