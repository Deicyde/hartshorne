# The denominator ideal of a birational line bundle

## Status

Lean candidate, pending review. Module `Hartshorne.Scheme.BirationalDenominatorIdeal`, imported by
the library root. Sorry-free; only the standard axioms.

The main theorem is `AlgebraicGeometry.Scheme.exists_mono_moduleSheafDual_tensor_pushforward`.
It proves two things:

- `f_* L` is a coherent sub-`O_X`-module of `K_X`, the constant sheaf `K(X)`;
- for `A` ample there is `n > 0` such that `A^{-n} ⊗ f_* L` is coherent and has a monomorphism to
  `O_X`.

So `A^{-n} ⊗ f_* L` is isomorphic to a coherent ideal sheaf of `O_X`, namely its image. Here
`A^{-n}` is `Hartshorne.Ringed.moduleSheafDual (moduleSheafTensorPower n A)`, the dual of the
`n`-th tensor power. It equals `Hartshorne.tensorZPow A (-n)` by definition, but `tensorZPow` is
recorded on the Cor 5.18 article, so the statement avoids it.

## Setting chosen for the Lean

- Varieties: integral schemes `X` and `Z`.
  - `X` is Noetherian and locally of finite type over a field `k`, via a structure morphism `pX`.
  - `[IsNoetherian X]` is needed because `AlgebraicGeometry.Scheme.Modules.IsAmple` is defined
    only on Noetherian schemes.
  - Separatedness and an algebraically closed field are not needed. Quasi-projectivity of `X`
    enters only through the given ample `A`.
- `f : Z ⟶ X` projective: `Hartshorne.Projective f`.
- Birational: `hf : f.base (genericPoint Z) = genericPoint X` together with
  `Function.Bijective (f.genericFunctionFieldMap hf)`, the pullback of rational functions
  `K(X) → K(Z)` (`AlgebraicGeometry.Scheme.Hom.genericFunctionFieldMap`).
  - A projective birational morphism of varieties over `k` satisfies this.
  - The Thm 7.17 chain has no scheme-level birational-morphism notion yet. A later wrapper may
    derive these hypotheses from whichever notion it settles on.
- `L` is any invertible sheaf on `Z`; the book's `L` is the line bundle of a projective embedding.
- The constant sheaf `K` is `X.totalQuotientModule`, the sheaf `K_X` of total quotient rings as an
  `O_X`-module. On an integral scheme its sections over an open set containing the generic point
  are `K(X)` (`totalQuotientSheafEquivFunctionField`).

## Proof outline and declarations (namespace `AlgebraicGeometry.Scheme`)

1. `f_* L ⊆ K_X`: `exists_pushforward_toTotalQuotientModule_injective`.
   - Proposition II.6.15 (`exists_invertibleTotalQuotientSubmodule_iso`) puts `L` inside `K_Z`.
   - `totalQuotientPushforward` (`f_* K_Z ⟶ K_X`, through the inverse of the function-field
     pullback) is injective on sections.
2. Coherence: `isCoherent_pushforward_of_isInvertible`, from Corollary II.5.20.
3. Common denominators: `exists_ne_zero_smul_mem_toK`. Over an affine `U ∋ η`, `F(U)` is finitely
   generated and `K(X) = Frac O_X(U)`.
4. The denominator ideal.
   - `denominatorIdealObj ι U = {a | a · ι(F(U)) ⊆ O_X(U)}`.
   - Compatibility with basic opens (`denominatorIdeal : X.IdealSheafData`) uses
     `exists_res_smul_eq_toK`, proved through stalks.
   - `denominatorIdealSheaf` is the corresponding quasi-coherent ideal sheaf. It is coherent,
     nonzero near `η`, and satisfies `J · F ⊆ O_X` on every open set
     (`exists_smul_eq_toK_of_mem_denominatorIdealSheaf`).
5. Ampleness: `exists_isGloballyGenerated_denominatorIdealSheaf_tensor`.
6. Twist: `exists_mono_moduleSheafDual_tensor_of_isGloballyGenerated`.
   - `J ⊗ P` has a nonzero section near `η`, using a frame of `P` and
     `IsLocalFrame.tensorFrameMap_bijective`.
   - So it has a nonzero global section `σ`.
   - Through `J ⊗ P ≅ ℋom(P^∨, J)` (`tensorIsoHomDual`) and
     `moduleSheafHomGlobalSectionsAddEquiv`, `σ` is a nonzero morphism `t : P^∨ ⟶ J`.
   - The morphism `F ⊗ P^∨ ⟶ O_X`, `s ⊗ ψ ↦ t(ψ) · s` (private `mulHom`), is built from a
     bilinear map.
   - It is mono: locally every section is `s ⊗ d` for a frame `d`, and `t(d) ≠ 0`
     (private `app_frame_ne_zero`, integrality), so `t(d) · ι(s) = 0` in the field `K(X)` forces
     `s = 0`.
7. `exists_denominatorIdealSheaf_of_birational` packages steps 1–4 for `F = f_* L`.

The book reads `σ` as an injection `A^{-n} → J` and then uses `A^{-n} ⊗ f_* L → J · f_* L ⊆ O_X`.
The Lean follows that route, with the composite built in one step from the bilinear map
`(s, ψ) ↦ t(ψ) · s`.
