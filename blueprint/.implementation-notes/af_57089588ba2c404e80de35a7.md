# Dominant maps are generically smooth on the source in characteristic zero

## Lean

`Hartshorne.exists_nonempty_open_smooth_of_isDominant` in
`src/Hartshorne/Cohomology/SmoothMorphisms/DominantCharZeroGenericallySmooth.lean`.
The schemes over `k` are given by structure morphisms `pX`, `pY` with
`f ≫ pY = pX`; finite type is `LocallyOfFiniteType ∧ QuasiCompact`, as in
`Hartshorne.SmoothTangent.smoothOfRelativeDimension_tfae_of_isRegularAt`.
"Smooth" is Mathlib's `Smooth`, as in the Frobenius example
(`Hartshorne.frobenius_projectiveLine_nowhere_smooth`).

## Route

The proof goes through Mathlib, not through the articles listed as
dependencies: a dominant map sends the generic point to the generic point, so
the stalk map at the generic point of `X` is `K(Y) → K(X)`, essentially of
finite type (`LocallyOfFiniteType.stalkMap`). `K(Y)` has characteristic zero
(it receives a ring map from `k`), hence is perfect, so
`Algebra.FormallySmooth.of_perfectField` applies. Then the generic point lies
in `f.smoothLocus` (open), and `Scheme.Hom.preimage_smoothLocus_eq` with
`Scheme.Hom.smoothLocus_eq_top_iff` shows `U.ι ≫ f` is smooth for
`U = f.smoothLocus`. Template: Mathlib's
`Scheme.Hom.genericPoint_mem_smoothLocus_of_perfectField`.

`[IsAlgClosed k]` is kept from the source but unused (Batteries'
`unusedArguments` reports it); the quasi-compactness halves of the finite-type
hypotheses are also not needed.

The image-of-generic-point step is inlined; the same fact exists as
`Hartshorne.map_genericPoint_of_isDominant` in
`Hartshorne.Scheme.GenericallyFiniteDenseOpen`, which is not on this article's
dependency path.

A possible strengthening, closer to Hartshorne's relative-dimension definition
of smoothness, would conclude `SmoothOfRelativeDimension n (U.ι ≫ f)` for
`n = dim X - dim Y`; that needs constancy of the fibre rank of the relative
differentials on the irreducible `U`
(`Hartshorne.Scheme.smoothOfRelativeDimension_of_smooth_of_finrank_fibre`).
