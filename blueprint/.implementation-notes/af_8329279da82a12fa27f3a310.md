# An ample twist makes relative Proj projective: implementation note

## Lean declarations

- `Hartshorne.RelativeProjData.projective_projection_and_isVeryAmpleRelative_of_isAmple`
  (module `Hartshorne.Scheme.RelativeProjAmpleTwist`): the article's statement. For
  `D : RelativeProjData X` (hypotheses (†) on `X`, `S`) and `L` ample, `Projective D.projection`
  and `∃ n₀, ∀ n ≥ n₀, IsVeryAmpleRelative D.projection (𝒪(1) ⊗ π^* L^n)`, with
  `𝒪(1) = D.algebra.relTwistingSheaf 1` and `L^n = moduleSheafTensorPower n L`.
- Supporting results (module `Hartshorne.Scheme.RelativeProjAmpleTwistEmbedding`):
  `Hartshorne.RelativeProjData.exists_isImmersion_twist` (for one `m`, an immersion
  `Proj_X S ⟶ ℙᴺ_X` over `X` pulling `𝒪(1)` back to `𝒪(1) ⊗ π^* L^m`),
  `Hartshorne.isVeryAmpleRelative_moduleSheafTensor_of_hom` (very ample tensor a sheaf with a
  morphism to projective space is very ample), `Hartshorne.IsVeryAmpleRelative.of_iso`,
  `Hartshorne.IsAmple.exists_hom_projectiveSpace_tensorPower` (high powers of an ample sheaf give
  morphisms to `ℙˢ_X` over `X`).
- Chart helpers (module `Hartshorne.Scheme.RelativeProjAmpleTwistCharts`).

## Route and divergence from the printed proof

Hartshorne replaces `S` by `S · L^n` (Lemma 7.9), globally generates the coherent sheaf
`S₁ ⊗ L^n` by finitely many sections, and gets a graded surjection `𝒪_X[x₀, …, x_N] → S · L^n`,
hence a closed immersion into `ℙᴺ_X` (proj-surjection-closed-immersion).

The project records a quasi-coherent graded algebra only through its sections over affine opens
(`QuasiCoherentGradedAlgebra`); there is no sheaf of modules `S₁` on `X` to which the ample
definition (`IsAmple`, phrased for coherent `X.Modules`) could be applied, and no global sections
of `S · L^n`. Building `S₁` as a coherent `X.Modules` (gluing the tilde modules of `S(U)₁` and
proving coherence and the comparison of global sections with compatible families) is the missing
infrastructure for the printed route.

The Lean instead works on `P = Proj_X S` directly:

1. Ampleness gives finitely many `w ∈ Γ(X, L^a)` with affine `X_w` covering `X`
   (`IsAmple.exists_isAffineOpen_nonvanishingLocus`), and `S(X_w)₁` is generated by finitely many
   `g_{w j}` (`DegreeOneCoherent`).
2. The degree-one sections `g_{w j}` of `𝒪(1)` over `π⁻¹(X_w)` are multiplied by a power of
   `π^* w` and extended to global sections of `𝒪(1) ⊗ (π^* L^a)^{⊗ n}`
   (`exists_forall_sectionPowSMul_extension`, the Lemma II.5.14 argument applied on `P`).
3. These sections generate, so Theorem 7.1 gives `φ : P ⟶ ℙᴺ_X` over `X`. Over each affine
   `π⁻¹(X_w) ∩ D₊(x_{w j})` of `ℙᴺ_X` the preimage is `D₊(g_{w j}) ⊆ Proj S(X_w)` and the ring map
   is onto `S(X_w)_(g_{w j})` (generated by `Γ(X_w)` and the `g_{w b}/g_{w j}`), so `φ` is an
   immersion; it is proper, hence a closed immersion. This gives projectivity and very ampleness
   for one exponent `m = a n`.
4. For `n = m + r` with `r` large, `L^r` is generated by finitely many global sections, giving
   `X ⟶ ℙˢ_X` over `X`; the Segre argument of Exercise 5.12(a) (only the first factor needs to
   be an immersion) shows `𝒪(1) ⊗ π^* L^n` is very ample.

Lemma 7.9 (`lineBundleTwist`) and the graded-surjection closed immersion are not used.
